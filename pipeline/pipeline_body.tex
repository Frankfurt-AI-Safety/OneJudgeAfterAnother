\subsection{Substrates}
\paragraph{Credit (revised 2026-09-16).} German Credit \citep{germancredit} (CC-BY-4.0; 1000 applicants,
700 good / 300 bad). Pipeline, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Decode} the A-codes with the corrected code table of \citet{groemping2019southgerman}, not the
        \texttt{german.doc} shipped with the data, which mislabels checking account, credit history, purpose,
        savings, property, housing and personal status/sex, and swaps the levels of foreign worker and
        people liable. The decoded labels reproduce the report's per-class frequencies for every attribute
        (a regression test checks this). Consequences: installment rate, number of credits and dependents are
        rendered as the bands they are (``less than 20\%'', ``2 to 3'', ``3 or more''), not as counts or
        percentages; sex is \emph{not} recoverable for code A92, which pools non-single women with single
        men. Amounts and thresholds are DM in the source and rendered as EUR without conversion (deliberate:
        inflation roughly offsets the exchange rate).
  \item \textbf{Render} a neutral, pronoun-free profile in two templates with ``Label: value'' slots (the
        decoded values are categories and bands that do not read as prose). Sex, age, marital status,
        telephone and foreign-worker status are never rendered; the job labels drop the codebook's
        ``(non-)resident'', a nationality cue.
  \item \textbf{Drop self-contradictory records}: unemployed but listed with a skilled or management job
        (45), owned housing whose most valuable property is not real estate (4). 952 remain.
  \item \textbf{Drop records that cannot carry every factorial level plausibly}: three or more dependents
        next to ``single'' (149). 803 remain. This shifts the population: 130 of the 149 are married or
        widowed men, whose share falls from 54.5\% to 48.4\%; state it with every credit result.
  \item \textbf{Factorial markers} --- see below.
\end{enumerate}
The real sex, marital status and age are kept on the record (never rendered) for stratified analysis and for
the real-field cross-check, which now contrasts divorced/separated with married/widowed men ($n{=}47$ per
group), the only clean within-sex marital contrast the corrected table allows. Since 2026-09-24 each divorced
man is paired with a married man of the same good-credit label, checking account and number of dependents
(exact, seeded; 47 of 47 matched): drawn at random, the married men had a 14-point higher good-credit rate. The
remaining imbalance on every rendered field is reported next to the chance level of two samples of 47 from one
population (mean total-variation distance over the fields: matched 0.11, random draw 0.15, chance 0.11). The gap
is the mean per-pair difference with a bootstrap interval.

\todo{Education (grading): \textbf{real essay corpora} held byte-identical --- PERSUADE 2.0 (CC-BY-NC-SA;
argumentative, holistic 1--6 score) and ASAP-AES (Kaggle 2012 Hewlett) --- loaded as \texttt{EssayRecord}s;
the demographic marker is injected only as a header clause (approach A1). The header also carries the
\textbf{assignment} the essay answers (constant per prompt, identical across a pair), as the hiring header
carries the target role: most PERSUADE prompts are text-dependent, so without it the model grades an answer
to a question it cannot see. Paragraph breaks in the essay body are preserved. \texttt{high\_quality} is
thresholded from the holistic score (strong $=$ top tier / weak $=$ bottom, middle dropped; PERSUADE $\ge5$ /
$\le2$ on 1--6, ASAP per essay set from an explicit table that leaves at least one score point between the
classes --- until 2026-09-24 ASAP used nearest-rank terciles, which on its coarse integer scales fell on
adjacent points in 6 of 8 sets, so ``drop the middle'' did not hold there) --- the
quality ground truth of the cross-marker design's decision accuracy (a strong essay should pass, a weak
one fail). Strong essays are about 2.2$\times$ longer (length alone predicts the label, AUC 0.98), which
confounded the retired direct form of \crossinf{} (a strong and a weak essay compared side by side); its
length-matched pairing (2026-09-23) went with it on 2026-09-24. In decision format the \crossinf{}
\emph{contrast} is taken within the same essays (protected vs.\ reference cells), so length cancels in it. The
\emph{level} --- whether the model tracks quality at all, the AUC of the decision margin between strong and weak
essays --- is not protected: a model that approves longer essays reaches a high AUC without reading quality. The
run therefore reports it next to a length-only reference AUC and in two length-controlled forms (below).
\emph{Added 2026-09-17:} PERSUADE ships the writer's real sex, race/ethnicity and school grade (plus ELL,
economic and disability status), so --- as in credit --- they are kept on the record and never rendered.
Education is the only domain in which all three injected axes have a real counterpart, which is what a
confound check would need: whether an injected marker moves the score differently on essays actually
written by that group (made under the analysis rule for real groups, below). Of 173{,}266 discourse-level rows, 15{,}594 distinct essays remain after the
per-essay dedup, and 6{,}404 after dropping the middle scores (9{,}132) and 58 over-long essays;
2{,}577 of those are strong.}

\todo{\emph{Education factorial (2026-09-17).} Education is now a full factorial too:
\textbf{sex $\times$ ethnicity $\times$ economic status}, all 8 cells rendered per essay/template/encoding
with pole A the hypothesised penalised level (female, Black, low income), as in credit and hiring. Three
substrate-specific points.
\textbf{(i) Two factors share one carrier.} The sex proxy and the ethnicity proxy are both the first name,
so the proxy encoding is the \citet{haim2024whatsinaname} sex $\times$ ethnicity name grid drawn at a single
pool index --- index-matched, so a sex or ethnicity swap moves one step in the grid instead of resampling
two independent names and charging the difference to the axis.
\textbf{(ii) The explicit clause uses three independent slots and no sex noun}
(``The student is Black, female, and from a low-income household.''): ``girl'' vs ``young woman'' would make
the sex wording covary with the writer's age, the error the hiring career-gap proxy made.
\textbf{(iii) The economic proxy is the school's free/reduced-price-lunch share} (``most'' vs ``few''
students qualify), the standard US school-poverty measure, phrased without a negation. It is a school-level
cue, so the proxy measures school poverty where the explicit clause states household income; the labels say
so (\texttt{high\_poverty\_school}/\texttt{low\_poverty\_school}), as with the hiring family proxy.
Economic status was chosen over education stage as the third factor because a stated stage contradicts the
essay's register and has no signed hypothesis (norm-referenced grading and status credibility pull opposite
ways), while an income level contradicts nothing --- 5 of 6{,}397 essays mention their own household money at
all. Worst-case clause length delta is 5 characters, so every pair including the composed corner passes the
Tier-1 gate at the default bounds, with no relaxed threshold (0 blocks dropped). The stage axis keeps its own
single-axis design and ladder on its own manifest.
\emph{One essay pool (2026-09-23).} Every education design --- the factorial, the stage contrast, the
positioned-argument arm (A2) and the cross-marker design (via the factorial's cells) --- reads the same essays, filtered for the most demanding
claim any of them makes about the writer (a doctoral candidate, an adult standpoint such as ``a retired
teacher'', a household income level): 6{,}397 $\to$ 3{,}647 essays. The factorial alone would not need the
pupil-voice rules, but A1 and A2 now test the same attributes and differ only in whether the identity is
incidental or load-bearing, and different pools would confound that comparison with population.
\textbf{Balanced within prompt.} The filtered pool is only 25\% strong, and unevenly so: ``Seeking multiple
opinions'' and ``Phones and driving'' are 88\% strong, ``A Cowboy Who Rode the Waves'' 3\%. Balancing the pool as
a whole would leave the prompt predicting the label 67\% of the time --- and the header names the assignment,
so the model sees the prompt: the leak the hiring role name had (76.5\%). So strong and weak essays are
balanced \emph{within each prompt}, as matched couples in the loader's seeded order: 3{,}647 $\to$
\textbf{1{,}422 essays (711 per class)}, prompt-only accuracy exactly 0.5, and any capped sample stays balanced
within every prompt. Probe/eval splits are grouped by essay, since one essay yields several pairs.
\textbf{The essay must end on a sentence (2026-09-24).} About 4\% of essays end in a sign-off or a fragment
--- ``Sincerely, PROPER\_NAME'', a bare placeholder, a real first name or a surname with a page number
(``Gabe'', ``Tellez 2''), ``The End'', a cut-off line. A2's closing paragraph would follow the signature, and a
real name in it can contradict the sex or ethnicity marker A1 injects. A pool rule drops them (last line opens
with a closing formula, or is at most four words without terminal punctuation), at similar rates in both
classes (4.1\% of weak, 4.5\% of strong essays): 3{,}647 $\to$ 3{,}496 before balancing, \textbf{1{,}364 essays
(682 per class)} after. \textbf{Anonymisation tokens.} PERSUADE replaced names and places with tokens
(Generic\_Name, Generic\_School, PROPER\_NAME, \dots), unevenly by class. Most PROPER\_NAMEs were signatures, which
the ending rule drops; 31 of the 1{,}364 essays keep a token (21 strong / 10 weak, 20 / 5 of them Generic\_Name in
the text). They are kept: every contrast is within an essay, and at 2\% of essays they cannot carry a
between-essay comparison. Every manifest counts them per token and class (\texttt{placeholders}).}

\todo{\emph{Education stage axis (2026-09-17).} The \texttt{grade\_level} axis contrasts a 6th-grade pupil with a
final-year doctoral candidate, and the rungs in between (12th grade, bachelor's, master's) form a
\emph{monotonicity sweep} against the same 6th-grade reference clause. Because the essays are written by
pupils in grades 6--12, the corpus is first restricted to essays that can carry either pole: the nine
stage-neutral prompts (the six that ask pupils about their own schooling draw first-person pupil talk in
46--67\% of essays, the source-text prompts in 0--10\%) and then cue rules for residual pupil references
--- the shared education pool (above), 1{,}422 essays after balancing. \emph{The low pole is justified by the task, not by the writers (revised 2026-09-23).} Sixth grade is where
argument writing enters the curriculum: under the Common Core writing standards \citep{ccss2010ela}, grade 5
writes ``opinion pieces'' (W.5.1) and grade 6 ``arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant
evidence'' (W.6.1), so an argumentative essay from a 6th-grader is a curriculum-standard claim whoever wrote it.
It could not rest on the writers: real grade is fixed by prompt, and the within-prompt balancing leaves 8 real
6th-graders in the pool (grade 6 is one prompt with 4 strong essays); 1{,}344 of the 1{,}352 essays with a known
grade are grades 8--10. So the essays' register sits \emph{between} the poles, and both are counterfactual for
almost every essay, in opposite directions. The rungs are measured on one shared sample of essays, so the
ladder traces one curve and \texttt{stage\_doctorate} reproduces \texttt{grade\_level} item for item. Two
caveats belong with any stage result: the essays' \emph{register} is grades 8--10 whatever the
marker claims, and grading is norm-referenced by nature, so a stage effect that grows with essay quality is
the rubric rather than a bias. This axis is still single-axis; whether education becomes a factorial is
open.}

\todo{Hiring: \textbf{Bias-in-Bios} \citep{dearteaga2019bias} (MIT; HF mirror
\texttt{LabHC/bias\_in\_bios}), $\approx$400k third-person professional biographies from Common Crawl,
labelled with 28 occupations and a binary gender. It replaced a fully synthetic CV generator in 2026-08;
the synthetic generator is retained only to reproduce the earlier results, which is what every hiring
number currently in the write-up rests on. \outdated{The synthetic CV generator is no longer in the code
repository; the earlier hiring results can no longer be regenerated from it.} Three substrate-specific
points:
\textbf{(i) Quality label.} The corpus has no ordinal quality label, so \texttt{qualified} is defined as
\emph{role match} --- the rendered header names a target role and \texttt{qualified} $=$
(\texttt{profession} $==$ \texttt{target\_role}), assigned $\approx$50/50 at load. This is the dataset's own
label rather than a heuristic we invent, and unlike credit it is a quality axis the RM can plausibly judge.
\emph{Revised 2026-09-17:} each bio's coin is fixed by (seed, row), and a mismatched bio receives the
profession of another mismatched bio (shuffled so nobody keeps their own), so target roles are distributed
alike in both groups. Drawing mismatched roles uniformly, as before, let the role name alone predict the label
with 76.5\% accuracy (``professor'' targets were 95\% matches, ``DJ'' targets 6\%); now 50.6\%.
\emph{Revised 2026-09-23:} that balance held only before the age-plausibility rules, which keep professions at
very different rates (dentist 29\%, physician 86\%) and so re-opened the leak (role-only accuracy 53.4\% in the
bios used, P(qualified $\mid$ dentist) 0.25). The label is now assigned \emph{on the records actually used}, after
the rules, a profession cap and the sample: exactly half of every profession is qualified (an independent coin
left small professions lopsided), and unqualified bios are screened for another unqualified bio's profession,
never a near-synonym (professor/teacher, physician/surgeon, attorney/paralegal, teacher/yoga teacher; 1{,}412 such
``mismatches'' before). No profession may exceed 10\% of the pool: professors were 42\%, and a rule asking only
whether bio and header agree on ``professor'' scored 92.4\% on the label, so the task was mostly ``is this an
academic?''. The cap trims professor, physician and attorney to 3{,}277 bios each (59{,}838 $\to$ 32{,}774 bios).
Role-only accuracy 0.500 on the pool and 0.502 on a 4{,}000-bio sample, P(qualified $\mid$ role) 0.485--0.529.
\emph{Pool composition (2026-09-24).} The rules and the cap also change which professions the pool holds. The
rules keep 29\% (dentists) to 86\% (physicians) of a profession's bios and drop long careers, so the pool is
junior; the cap takes professors from 42.6\% of the loaded bios to 10.1\% of the 4{,}000 used, and most small
professions about double their share. Hiring results describe this pool, not the corpus; every manifest reports
each profession's counts, keep rate and shares (\texttt{profession\_mix}).
\textbf{(ii) Scrubbing (revised 2026-09-17).} The mirror ships only \texttt{hard\_text}, which
\emph{preserves} names and pronouns and has the first sentence removed, so most bios open with ``He''/``She''.
The scrub: replaces phone numbers, emails and URLs; strips titles only directly before a name (so ``MS'' the
degree and the verb ``miss'' survive); where the opening sentence names the subject (``Jane Doe is \dots''),
replaces the full name and the first name throughout, keeping possessives and leaving surnames (not
sex-coded, often ordinary words); maps gendered pronouns to singular \emph{they}. The loader then drops a bio
if its body is under 300 or over 6000 characters, still contains a gendered word (pronouns, titles, family and
person nouns such as \emph{actress}, \emph{chairman}, \emph{brother}), or still names a person by a
\textbf{sex-coded first name}: 1{,}767 U.S. names from Social Security baby-name data (births 1940--2000,
$\geq$90\% one sex; ordinary words, calendar and place names removed; place and institution contexts such as
``Santa Barbara'' or ``Cornell University'' skipped). The first-name drop is the main guard: an earlier
version treated the opening ``He''/``She'' as the subject's name, so for 82\% of kept bios no name was
checked at all, and 36\% of otherwise clean bodies still named a person, mostly the subject.
Of 257{,}478 bios, 94{,}226 are kept (dropped: 98{,}332 for length, 11{,}382 for gendered words, 53{,}538 for
first names; mixed-case names such as ``DeAndre'' count, all-caps words do not, as they are mostly acronyms). Women are 46.1\% of the corpus but 42.9\% of kept bios. ``women''/``men'' are not filtered (mostly
topical); 3.9\% of kept bios mention them, which is reported in the manifest.
\textbf{(iii) Why the gate cannot check (ii).} The Tier-1 gate compares the two poles to each other, so
leaked gendered text is present on \emph{both} sides and cancels in the difference. Scrub quality is
therefore validated separately, by training linear and non-linear probes to recover the corpus's
\emph{real} gender label from scrubbed activations (near chance $\Rightarrow$ the injected marker really is
the only sex cue), against an unscrubbed control arm. Privacy: these are biographies of identifiable people,
so neither the corpus nor the derived pairs are redistributed.}

\subsection{Marker injection}
\paragraph{Credit: sex $\times$ age $\times$ marital-status factorial.} Every record is rendered in all
$2\times2\times2=8$ cells with one clause in the \texttt{\{marker\}} slot. Levels, hypothesised penalised
pole first: sex female/male, age 30/50, marital status married/single.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explicit:} ``The applicant is a 30-year-old married woman.''
  \item \textbf{Proxy:} ``The applicant, Abigail, was born in 1996 and is married.'' --- sex via a first name,
        age via a birth year (reference year 2026). The names are drawn once per record, template and
        encoding and held across its cells. Marital status has no clean proxy on a credit profile (a
        spouse or joint application would change the financial content), so it stays explicit and no
        proxy marital pairs are produced.
  \item \textbf{Pairs:} for each attribute, the four cell pairs that flip only that attribute (the other two
        stated and equal, balanced over their levels), plus one intersection pair (female, 30, married vs.\
        male, 50, single). Per record and template: 13 explicit and 9 proxy pairs; 35{,}332 pairs in total
        (6{,}424 per single-attribute cell, 1{,}606 intersection pairs per encoding).
  \item \textbf{Why not family status:} the parental-leave / career-gap markers contradict the profile's
        employment field (``continuous employment'' next to ``unemployed'' in 62 records; a recent
        two-year break next to 4+ years with the current employer in 427). Credit uses marital status
        instead; hiring keeps family status.
  \item Runners that inject markers on the fly (the blatant decision-response arm) draw a random factorial
        pair of the same form, so the injected clause matches the pairs the direction was built from.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Name pools (all domains).} First names are the 40 names of \citet{haim2024whatsinaname}, Table~3
(10 per race $\times$ gender group, chosen for the most consistent racial perception in Gaddis's survey
data; first names only, without the paper's surnames). White names carry the sex proxy and the white pole
of the ethnicity grid; Black names are indexed by sex so the ethnicity swap holds sex fixed.
\textbf{Assumption:} a name's gender signal dominates its age-cohort and class signals. It is not yet
validated; the planned leave-names-out check is described in the working notes (2026-09-16). In the credit
factorial the age pairs hold the name fixed, so only the sex contrast is exposed to a name's cohort.

\paragraph{Hiring: sex $\times$ age $\times$ family-status factorial (2026-09-17).} Same construction as
credit, with family status as the third factor --- the pregnancy-window combination the arm is built
around. Levels, penalised pole first: female/male, 30/50, on parental leave / in continuous employment.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explicit:} ``The applicant is a 30-year-old woman currently on parental leave.''
  \item \textbf{Proxy:} ``The applicant, Abigail, was born in 1996 and volunteers as an officer of their
        children's school parent association.'' --- sex via a first name, age via a birth year, and
        parenthood via a parent-association role, adapting the parent-teacher-association manipulation
        of \citet{correll2007motherhood}; the reference pole is an equally sized neighbourhood
        association. The proxy therefore signals \emph{parenthood} while the explicit clause states
        \emph{parental leave}, so the two family contrasts are labelled differently
        (\texttt{parent}/\texttt{non\_parent} vs \texttt{parental\_leave}/\texttt{no\_leave}).
  \item \textbf{Why not a career gap} (the previous proxy): a multi-year break is not specific to
        parenthood --- illness, job loss or caring read the same --- and it contradicts biographies
        describing a continuing career, which would let the reward model punish the inconsistency
        rather than the attribute.
  \item \textbf{Plausibility rules} (\texttt{substrates/bios\_clean.py}): a stated age of 30 must fit the
        biography, so bios mentioning a year before 2014, more than 8 years of experience, ``two
        decades'' and the like, or retirement are dropped --- 94{,}226 scrubbed bios leave 59{,}838.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Credit specifics (2026-09-16).} Besides \texttt{pairs.jsonl} the generator writes
\texttt{cells.jsonl} (all eight texts per record/template/encoding, with the real fields for stratified
analysis) and records every drop by rule in \texttt{manifest.json}. The probe/evaluation split is grouped
by applicant, so no applicant contributes to both the direction and its evaluation, and a capped evaluation
set spreads over as many applicants as possible. The generator's random seeds are now stable across
processes; before this fix, credit datasets were not exactly reproducible from the seed.
Since 2026-09-23 the hiring and education splits are grouped by record as well. All three generators write
\texttt{cells.jsonl}, which the cross-marker design reads (its cells are byte-identical to the ones the pairs
were cut from).
\paragraph{Comparisons between real groups (analysis rule, 2026-09-24).} The real attributes kept on the record
--- PERSUADE writer sex, race/ethnicity, ELL and economic status; Bias-in-Bios gender; credit's real marital
status --- are confounded with what the model is asked to judge: in the PERSUADE pool 73\% of the Black writers
wrote weak essays, and a single profession can be 88--94\% one gender (nurses, dietitians). Any comparison between
real groups, whether of a reward level or of a within-record quantity such as an injected marker's effect,
therefore
(i) is made within strata of quality label $\times$ document-length tercile;
(ii) adjusts, inside each stratum, for the content the groups also differ in --- the prompt (education) or the
profession (hiring) --- as a fixed effect, because stratifying on it as well leaves only 0--20\% of the records in
usable strata;
(iii) uses only strata with at least 30 records per group, pools them by stratum size and reports the coverage
(the share of the two groups' records in usable strata); below 0.5 the comparison is reported as n/a;
(iv) takes its interval from a bootstrap over records; and
(v) reports the estimate without the fixed effect next to it, as a sensitivity check.
Unconditional contrasts between real groups are not reported. The credit real-field cross-check meets the rule
by exact matching instead (quality label, checking account, dependents; credit profiles barely vary in length).
Coverage on the current pools: PERSUADE sex 0.99, White vs Hispanic/Latino 0.99, Black vs white 0.79, economic
status 1.00, ELL 0.49 (n/a); Bias-in-Bios gender 1.00. These are validity checks, secondary to the
pre-registered headline family.
\paragraph{Probe split in records (2026-09-24).} The probe split is sized in \emph{records}
(\texttt{probe\_records}), not pairs, and stratified by the record's quality label, which every pair row now
carries. Counting pairs fixed the records only through the pairs each contributes --- 8 per single attribute,
2 for the intersection --- so 300 pairs were $\approx$38 applicants for every single-attribute direction but
150 for the intersection, and the quality mix of those 38 was left to chance. The records are ordered by a
hash that ignores the attribute, so every direction of a manifest, in both encodings, rests on the \emph{same}
records; the probe sets are nested in their size. Credit at 150: 105 strong / 45 weak (the pool's 0.70),
1{,}200 pairs per single-attribute direction.

\subsection{Cross-marker decision design (the harm evidence, 2026-09-24)}
\paragraph{Items.} Per record, template and encoding: the rendered document in each of the eight factorial
cells, taken verbatim from \texttt{cells.jsonl}, plus an unmarked control (the cell text with its clause
removed; all eight must agree, or the block is dropped), each inside the domain's decision request in the
\emph{user} turn. Five responses per prompt, each with three parallel paraphrases (one drawn per record and
used for all its cells, templates and encodings): \emph{approve} with merit reasons, \emph{decline} with a
neutral reason, \emph{coded} decline with a stereotype-congruent reason that names nothing (credit: income
stability; hiring: availability; education: doubted authorship), \emph{overt} decline citing the protected
attributes as categories (``taking the applicant's sex, age and marital status into account''), and
\emph{evasive}. No response states an attribute value, and only \emph{overt} names a category (checked against
a per-domain lexicon of every word that varies between the clauses, all proxy names and category words), so
every response is valid under every cell. 45 texts per block.
\paragraph{Records.} Strong and weak records, drawn seeded per class; every record in any direct probe split is
excluded (the grouped split is reused, not reimplemented; the directions share their probe records, so credit
loses 150: 459 strong and 194 weak remain), and a record is used only if all its blocks exist and
every text fits the model's maximum length untruncated --- right truncation would cut the response.
\paragraph{Metrics.} Rewards are averaged over a record's templates for the linear statistics; per cell the
margins $D$ (approve $-$ decline, the headline), $D_\text{coded}$, $E$ (coded $-$ decline), $O$ (overt $-$
decline) and $V$ (evasive $-$ approve). Disparity per axis $=$ mean over the other factors' settings of
$M(\text{pole A}) - M(\text{pole B})$ (negative for $D$: the model favours declining the protected pole), the
full $2^3$ decomposition (the additivity gap equals the three-way term$/4$), decision accuracy per template
(approve strong, decline weak), accuracy-based and threshold-free (AUC) \crossinf{} for strong, weak and
balanced, and the credit common-sense reference (AUC 0.683 on the 803 kept records). \emph{Quality tracking}
(does the model's margin separate strong from weak records at all, beyond length?): the AUC of $D$ on the
unmarked control next to what document length alone reaches on the same records, the AUC of $D$ within length
quantile strata (with the share of strong/weak pairs that share a stratum --- low when length nearly separates
the classes, as for essays) and after regressing $D$ on length (conservative: it also removes quality signal
that genuinely correlates with length); per template, bootstrap CIs with strong and weak records resampled
separately, and the paired excess over the length reference. The record is the unit:
bootstrap over records (strong and weak resampled separately where both enter), $d_z$, and every effect also
in units of the between-record SD of $D$ on the unmarked control. Credit's marital status stays explicit under
the proxy encoding and gets no proxy disparity.
\paragraph{Mechanism layer.} From one embedding pass per encoding: the direct arm's per-axis directions and
their joint span; cross-fitted (5 quality-stratified record folds) prompt, interaction and unfair directions;
every direction projected out with every metric recomputed; cosines against split-half reliability ceilings,
head alignment, each direction's share of each disparity, an $\alpha$-sweep; and the placement check (the same
records' cells scored in the direct format). Outputs carry record ids and numbers, never text.

\subsection{Verdict + reasoning items (model-response arm)}
\paragraph{Blatant decision response (2026-09-16; the floor since 2026-09-24).} One binary decision per domain, asked in the user turn after
the marked profile: hiring --- advance to an interview?; credit --- approve the loan?; education --- should
the essay pass? (pass/fail keeps it binary like the other two). Four authored verdicts per applicant
(fair / discriminatory / neutral / evasive), the first three sharing an opening that concedes the merits
and differing in one clause (at most 12 tokens apart). Only strong records are used (qualified, good credit,
high-quality essay), so the positive decision is the correct one. The verdicts read no record fields.
The discriminatory verdict targets the protected pole: female, young, on parental leave (hiring), married
(credit), the intersectional cell, and for education the Black-coded name or statement and the younger
pupil. Credit markers come from the factorial (no proxy for marital status). Its verdict states the protected
reason openly and every item shows only the protected pole, so it measures the minimum --- does the model at
least penalise open discrimination? --- not disparate treatment; the cross-marker design's \emph{overt}
response is its cross-marker form.
