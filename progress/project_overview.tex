\noindent A \rewardmodel{} (RM) is the shared evaluator of modern alignment: one preference model is trained
once, then reused during RLHF, best-of-$N$ selection, and data filtering. A systematic error in that single
component is not a local defect --- it propagates into everything trained against it.

\section*{The method in short}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item An RM is a transformer backbone plus \emph{one linear head} producing a single number:
        $r = \mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{h} + b$, where $\mathbf{h}$ is the hidden state at the last token.
        The reward is a linear readout of one vector --- that is the leverage.
  \item We build \textbf{matched pairs}: texts that are byte-identical except for one swapped attribute
        (enforced by a gate: single-slot difference, length and readability parity).
  \item Averaging each pole's hidden states and subtracting gives a unit \diffmean{} direction $\mathbf{v}$
        --- the displacement in activation space caused by flipping the attribute.
  \item \textbf{\nullspace{}} removes the component along it:
        $\mathbf{h}' = \mathbf{h} - \alpha\,(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{h})\,\mathbf{v}$, with $\alpha$ a dial
        ($0$ = off, $1$ = bias component exactly zero).
  \item The reward then shifts by exactly $-\alpha\,(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{h})(\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{v})$:
        how far \emph{this} input sits along the bias axis, times how much the head \emph{cares} about it.
        We are not penalising the output; we are severing the channel the bias travels through.
  \item \textbf{No weights are modified} --- an activation edit at scoring time, between the pooled hidden
        state and the scalar head:
\end{itemize}
\begin{verbatim}
  tokens -> backbone -> last-layer hidden state -> last token -> [ PROJECTION ]
         -> scalar head -> reward
\end{verbatim}

\section*{Step 1 --- \textit{One Bias After Another} (base paper, arXiv:2603.03291)}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item Established the mechanistic instrument above, and the distinction between \emph{low-complexity} bias
        (a near-linear direction removable at little cost) and \emph{high-complexity} bias (entangled with
        legitimate signal).
  \item Applied it to capability- and style-related biases: response length, answer position, sycophancy,
        and uncertainty.
  \item What it did \emph{not} do: it was never applied to protected attributes.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Step 2 --- What we are doing now}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item Carrying that lens into \textbf{protected-attribute and intersectional bias}, across three EU AI Act
        high-risk domains: credit scoring, hiring, and essay grading.
  \item A matched-pair generator with a structural validation gate, plus a stronger robustness test:
        \leace{} (provably optimal linear erasure) followed by a \emph{non-linear} probe --- the \taco{}
        signature of entanglement.
  \item Findings on \modelSmall{}: demographic effects are large and behaviourally removable (explicit sex
        on hiring, \autoInflSexCVbase{} $\rightarrow$ \autoInflSexCVnull{} when nulled), and a novel
        \emph{standpoint-credibility} effect appears, where the RM rewards an identical argument more when
        voiced from a marginalised standpoint (\standpointGapIntersectionPersuade{}).
  \item The headline mechanistic result: after \leace{} the linear probe drops to chance
        (\erasureLinearLeaceCorrPL{}) while an MLP still recovers the concept (\erasureMlpLeaceCorrPL{}).
        \textbf{Nulling fixes the reward, not the representation.} (Measured so far on the reasoning concepts
        of the model-response arm; the same test on the demographic directions is our next experiment.)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Step 3 --- What could be done next}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item Every result so far concerns the \emph{evaluator}. The open question is whether a debiased evaluator
        actually yields a less biased \emph{policy} --- whether the intervention has downstream value.
  \item Since the projection is a pure function of the hidden state, the debiased RM is a \textbf{drop-in
        reward function}: usable inside PPO with no retraining, via a thin wrapper around the score head.
        The RM stays frozen while the policy updates, so the direction estimated offline stays valid.
  \item This gives a \textbf{matched two-arm design} --- same base policy, seeds, and prompts, with the
        \emph{only} difference being raw vs.\ nulled reward --- isolating the activation edit as the sole
        causal variable. The prediction is falsifiable: the policy should improve on the nulled behaviour,
        while bias may persist on adjacent ones.
  \item New challenge to be honest about: benchmarking a \emph{policy} is a different measurement problem
        from scoring fixed text. It needs generation-side instruments and a capability guardrail, so that
        ``less biased'' is not simply ``worse''.
\end{itemize}
