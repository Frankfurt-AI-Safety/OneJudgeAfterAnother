\section{Research questions (draft)}
\label{sec:rq-draft}
Draft RQ set for the extension. Not yet locked; promote to \texttt{sections/} once settled.

\paragraph{Main question.}
Do language \RMs{} inherit EU-protected demographic (and intersectional) biases; are these biases
low- or high-complexity in the model's activations; can mechanistic reward shaping remove them; and is
that removal \emph{robust} --- or does the bias only get cleaned from the reward scalar while remaining
entangled in the representation and leaking back under new conditions?

\subsection{RQ0 --- Replication, and re-testing the base paper's own complexity claims}
Two limbs. \textbf{(a)} Do the base-paper low-complexity interventions (length, uncertainty, position)
reproduce on the eleven \RMs{}, with RewardBench-2 non-inferiority \citep{fein2026onebias}? \textbf{(b)}
Apply \leace{} $+$ non-linear-probe (\taco{}) to the base paper's \emph{own} identified biases (the
``low-complexity'' length/uncertainty/position and the ``high-complexity'' sycophancy/model-style): does
\nullspace{} remove the concept from the \emph{representation}, or only its contribution to the reward
\emph{scalar}? I.e.\ re-run the base paper's low/high-complexity classification under the stronger
erasure/recoverability test, and check the reward-vs-representation gap for the spurious biases too ---
not just the demographic ones. (This makes our robustness check a contribution back to the base method.)

\subsection{RQ1 --- Prevalence: which protected attributes are inherited?}
Which EU-protected attributes does an \rewardmodel{} exhibit a measurable bias for when scoring
otherwise-identical high-risk decision content across three Annex-III domains --- recruitment
(CV screening), credit scoring, and education (grading student assignments/tests)? Measured on
matched-pair contrastive items under explicit and proxy encodings, across the eleven \RMs{}. We report
magnitude and removability, not a betted direction. \emph{Since 2026-09-24} (methodology decision in the log):
the behavioural answer rests on the cross-marker decision design --- the disparity of the decision margin
$D = r(\text{approve}) - r(\text{decline})$ between protected and reference cells of the same record, and
decision-format \crossinf{} (reliability harm, \citealp{kumar2025prefix}); direct-scoring \autoinf{} shows
sensitivity under controlled substitution and belongs to the mechanism layer.

\paragraph{RQ1.1 --- Intersectional.}
For the anchor intersection (sex $\times$ age $\times$ family-status; women $\approx 30$)
\outdated{credit now uses sex $\times$ age $\times$ \emph{marital status}, see log 2026-09-16}, is there a
\emph{distinct} intersectional bias, and is it \emph{non-additive} --- larger than, and not equal to, the
vector sum of the single-attribute directions?

\subsection{RQ2 --- Downstream harm (decision-response)}
When the \rewardmodel{} scores a \emph{decision response} rather than the applicant text, would it reward a
policy model that discriminates (the RLHF / best-of-N propagation story)? \emph{Since 2026-09-24} answered by
the cross-marker decision design: the same record in all eight factorial cells in the user turn, responses
that state no attribute value (approve / neutral decline / coded decline / overt decline / evasive), and the
question whether the RM favours declining one group more than another on the identical document --- silent
disparate treatment, which is what a policy optimised against the RM would learn. The blatant arm (a fair vs
an openly discriminatory verdict, protected pole only; implemented 2026-09-16 in all three domains) is kept as
the \textbf{floor}: does the RM at least penalise openly stated discrimination? Its \textbf{evasion control}
(a non-committal answer must not out-score a substantive one) carries over to both.

\subsection{RQ3 --- Complexity: low vs high, and where it lives}
For each attribute and the intersection, is the bias low- or high-complexity, distinguishing two senses:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{reward-scalar level:} is its contribution to the scalar reward a single near-linear
        direction that \nullspace{} removes at near-zero cost to legitimate accuracy?
  \item \textbf{representation level:} after provably-optimal linear erasure (\leace{}
        \citep{belrose2023leace}), can a non-linear (MLP) probe still recover the concept? Linearly
        erasable but non-linearly recoverable $=$ the \taco{} signature of an entangled (high-complexity)
        bias \citep{taco2024}. This upgrades the base paper's nulling-based classification.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ4 --- Mitigation via mechanistic reward shaping}
Where the bias is (reward-scalar) low-complexity, does \diffmean{}/\leace{} + \nullspace{} reduce the
reliability harm and preference bias \emph{without} degrading legitimate reward accuracy (RewardBench-2
non-inferiority)? Report the trade-off curve over projection strength $\alpha$.

\subsection{RQ5 --- Robustness / generalization of the intervention}
Because reward shaping removes the bias's linear contribution to the scalar but not the entangled concept
from the representation (RQ3), how robust is the mitigation? Does a projection fit on one setting stay
effective on held-out domains, templates, encodings (explicit$\leftrightarrow$proxy), phrasings, and
unseen intersections --- or does the bias concept \emph{leak back} into the reward scalar under
distribution shift? (Operationalized: fit on split/domain A, measure residual bias $+$ accuracy on
held-out split/domain B; cross-domain and cross-encoding transfer.) The three domains --- credit,
recruitment (CV), and education (grading) --- give us a genuine cross-domain transfer test. \emph{Since
2026-09-24} also cross-\emph{placement}: does a direction fitted with the marker in the response (direct
arm) remove the disparity when the marker is in the request (cross-marker design), and which direction
carries the disparity at all (the exact decomposition $-(w\cdot u)(\Delta_\text{int}\cdot u)$, log stage 4)?

\subsection{RQ6 --- Cross-model consistency: dataset- vs architecture-driven}
Is the bias --- magnitude and low/high-complexity class --- consistent across the eleven \RMs{}, or does it
vary by model? Our set is a natural experiment with the controlled comparisons built in: the Skywork-V2
models share Skywork's preference data but differ in base architecture/size (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B,
Qwen3-0.6B, Gemma-2-27B), the AllenAI RB2 pair holds the recipe fixed across 8B and 70B (a clean size
comparison), the two Nemotron-32B models hold the recipe fixed across base generations, and
OpenAssistant-DeBERTa (encoder) plus QRM-Gemma (quantile/distributional) vary the RM \emph{type} itself. \outdated{DeBERTa most likely removed; the RM-type contrast would rest on QRM-Gemma
vs its own backbone, Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B-v0.2 --- see log 2026-09-24.}
If the bias clusters by \emph{training data} it is
dataset-driven; if by \emph{architecture/family}, architecture-driven \citep{kumar2025prefix}. This
locates where mitigation must live (upstream data governance vs model-internal editing).

\subsection{Validity confounds to pre-empt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reasoning-correctness (construct validity).} Is the measured effect really the protected
        attribute, or the RM tracking (correct) reasoning / response quality? We have evidence it is the
        latter for verdicts (reasoning-flip 2$\times$2 with a non-demographic commute control). Needs to
        be a stated control, not a footnote.
  \item \textbf{Brittleness.} RMs react to non-demographic perturbations too; matched pairs must hold all
        non-target variation fixed (our Tier-1 gate).
  \item \textbf{Evasion.} Fairness framing can manifest as non-answers; score for evasion so it does not
        masquerade as fairness.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deferred / potential future directions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Group-level (demographic-parity) framing \citep{song2025groupfairness} as a complement to the
        same-content counterfactual design --- do the two fall on opposite sides of the complexity
        boundary? \emph{Deferred}: out of scope for now, since it needs a \emph{new data pipeline} (group
        construction from genuinely different prompts, e.g.\ arXiv-style occupational proxies) rather than
        our matched-pair generator.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Design axes (factors, not RQs)}
These modulate RQ1--RQ4 rather than being separate questions:
domain $\{$credit, recruitment (CV), education (grading)$\}$; task framing
$\{$economic, fairness, neutral$\}$; encoding $\{$explicit, proxy$\}$; scoring readout
$\{$scalar matched-pair (primary), forced-choice pairwise (secondary cross-check)$\}$.

\subsection{Protected-attribute scope}
Ground the attribute list in EU Charter Art.~21 $+$ AI Act Art.~10(2)(f,g), Art.~15 \citep{euaiact2024}.
Anchors are per-domain: credit and recruitment (CV) anchor on the sex $\times$ age $\times$ family-status
intersection (the pregnancy window) \outdated{credit: sex $\times$ age $\times$ marital status as a full
factorial, since family-status markers contradict the credit profile's employment field}; \textbf{education (grading)} anchors instead on a header
\textbf{name} ($\to$ sex $+$ ethnicity name-proxy) and \textbf{grade level} ($\to$ age / education-stage),
injected on real essays; disability is deferred. All are drawn from the same scope below.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explicitly statable --- cleanest matched pairs:} sex, age, family/parental status,
        \textbf{disability}. Each can be a single-slot stated marker (``a woman'', ``50 years old'',
        ``on parental leave'', ``has a disability'') plus a proxy form --- a true single-axis swap.
  \item \textbf{Proxy-only (name-based) --- standard but confounded:} ethnic/national origin. There is
        \emph{no} clean stated form on a CV/loan profile; the audit-study method swaps validated
        \emph{name} lists coded to different origins \citep{haim2024whatsinaname}. Caveat: names conflate
        ethnicity with class, region, and religion, so the single-axis guarantee is weaker than for
        sex/age --- report it as a name-proxy effect, not pure ethnicity.
  \item \textbf{Hard to operationalize without artifice --- exploratory:} religion, sexual orientation.
        Explicit markers are unnatural on these profiles and proxies are noisy.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reward models under evaluation}
The reward-model (RM) set is chosen to disentangle whether bias is \emph{dataset-driven} or
\emph{architecture-driven}, with controlled comparisons built in: the same Skywork-V2 training data across
different base architectures (Llama-8B vs Qwen3-8B); the same training recipe across a large size gap (the
AllenAI RB2 8B vs 70B); distinct RM \emph{types} (a DeBERTa encoder RM and a quantile/distributional QRM);
and a frontier 70B flagship. \textbf{All reported runs go on the hessian.AI cluster (A100/H100 80\,GB),}
under a two-month allocation secured 2026-08. The Apple Silicon (MLX) path is retained for local prototyping
and as the numerical parity reference, not as a source of published numbers. \outdated{The MLX backend has
since been removed from the code repository; CUDA is the only path.} Everything routes through the
same probe / null-space / LEACE pipeline, unchanged per model. The \textbf{GPUs} column gives the bf16
scheduling requirement: everything up to 32B fits on a single 80\,GB card; the two 70B models need
$\approx$140\,GB and are sharded across two.

\subsection*{Validity workstream (item 2 delivered 2026-08-12)}
Three checks on whether our \emph{instruments} measure what we claim. These bear on how much of the A1 marker
evidence survives, so they are scheduled ahead of further breadth.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Real vs.\ synthetic marker validity --- promote from footnote to finding.} On the one real-field
        run (German Credit \texttt{personal\_status\_sex}, $n{=}142$/group, probe acc $0.92$) the real-field
        direction is \emph{anti}-correlated with our injected-marker directions: $\cos = -0.216$ against the
        synthetic family-status direction and $-0.297$ against the synthetic sex direction, with a real-field
        reward gap of $\approx 0$. Plan: extend to PERSUADE~2.0's unused real race/sex/ELL labels and to
        further credit fields; report $\cos(\text{real}, \text{synthetic})$ per axis; treat a persistent
        negative or near-zero cosine as a result \emph{about the injected-marker method itself}.
        \outdated{Void (log 2026-09-16): the run decoded \texttt{personal\_status\_sex} with the wrong
        code table, so its ``single'' men were married/widowed men and its comparison group mixed divorced
        men with single women. Sex is not even recoverable for the largest code. The corrected run contrasts
        divorced/separated with married/widowed men ($n{=}47$ per group) against the synthetic
        \emph{marital-status} direction; not yet run.}
        \emph{Now cheaper:} item 2 brings a second corpus with a real sex label, so this is measurable on
        hiring as well as credit without any new annotation. \emph{Since 2026-09-24} every such comparison
        follows the analysis rule for real groups (log 2026-09-24): real labels are confounded with quality,
        length and prompt or profession.
  \item \textbf{Bias-in-Bios real CVs --- \textsc{done}.} The hiring domain now loads Bias-in-Bios
        \citep{dearteaga2019bias} instead of the synthetic CV generator (4000 matched pairs, 8 axis
        $\times$ encoding cells, 0\% gate discard). Quality is \emph{role match} (true occupation $==$ the
        target role named in the header), so hiring gains a quality axis the RM can plausibly judge --- the
        thing credit lacks. \textbf{Caveat that matters:} the substrate changed, the evidence has not. The
        erasure, reasoning and decision arms have \emph{not} been re-run, so every hiring number in the
        write-up still rests on synthetic CVs. \textbf{Open sub-task:} the corpus preserves names and
        pronouns, so bodies are scrubbed; the Tier-1 gate structurally cannot verify that (leaked text sits
        on both poles and cancels), so \texttt{validate\_bios\_scrub.py} probes the corpus's \emph{real}
        gender label out of scrubbed activations. Near chance $\Rightarrow$ clean. That check has not been
        run on \modelSmall{} yet, and no sex-axis number from this substrate should be trusted until it is.
        \outdated{The check has since been run (review site: +0.10 over chance, about half of it the
        occupational prior), but on the scrub before 2026-09-17; re-run on the revised scrub.}
  \item \textbf{Name-pool validation} against Bertrand--Mullainathan / Haim et al.\ before any publishable
        ethnicity number. \texttt{markers.py} already self-flags the pools as an unvalidated starter set, and
        ethnicity is exactly the axis that is template-unstable across corpora
        (\eduEthnicityExplicitAsap{} ASAP vs \eduEthnicityExplicitPersuade{} PERSUADE).
        \emph{Done 2026-09-16:} the pools are now Haim et al.'s Table~3 (40 names). Remaining gap: the
        assumption that a name's gender signal dominates its age and class signals --- see the log entry
        of that date for the planned check. \emph{Pool level done 2026-09-26} (log entry of that date): sex
        pools balanced; the ethnicity pools differ strongly in class.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{2026-07-04 --- positioned-argument (A2) arm: standpoint-credibility bias}
A2 makes identity \emph{load-bearing for the argument's authority}: a first-person positionality sentence
grounds a real PERSUADE essay's conclusion in the author's lived experience, and we swap only the claimed
identity (``as \{a woman / a man / a Black person / \dots / a working-class Black woman / an affluent white
man\} who has lived these realities firsthand, I am convinced this is the right conclusion''), holding the
essay body byte-identical. Injection position is a config parameter (v1 $=$ conclusion). We add a
\textbf{non-demographic control} family (gardener/cyclist, dog/cat, rural/city) and a \textbf{neutral
(no-positionality) baseline}, and decompose each axis into a \emph{main effect} (any standpoint vs neutral)
and a magnitude \emph{identity gap} ($\Delta_a-\Delta_b$). \textbf{Result (\modelSmall{}, PERSUADE,
$n=160$):}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{General sycophancy for lived experience:} the main effect is positive on every axis
        ($+0.14$ to $+0.38$) --- the RM rewards \emph{any} ``I have lived this'' appeal over the plain essay,
        even hobby/pet.
  \item \textbf{A genuine, demographic-specific pro-marginalized bias:} the identity gap is large on the
        demographic axes (sex $+0.20$, race $+0.22$, class $+0.36$, origin $+0.28$, \textbf{intersection
        $+0.70$}) while the \emph{clean neutral controls are $\approx 0$} (gardener-vs-cyclist $-0.01$,
        dog-vs-cat $+0.02$). Direction is pro-marginalized (woman$>$man, Black$>$white, working-class$>$
        wealthy, immigrant$>$native), strongest for the working-class Black woman.
  \item \textbf{Methodological caution:} auto-influence \emph{saturates} (direction-consistency, not
        magnitude --- a trivial $+0.09$ gap gave auto-influence $0.80$), and an authority-tinged control
        (``retired teacher'') is contaminated. \textbf{Report the identity-gap magnitude against clean
        controls, not auto-influence, for A2.}
\end{itemize}
Deferred: opening/middle/random positions; referee (policy-model-grades $\to$ RM-rewards) framing; ASAP
replication; social-issue corpus where standpoint is topic-intrinsic; 8B RM.

\subsection*{2026-07-05 --- A2 hardening: robustness + stance control}
The pro-marginalized identity gap survives four robustness checks (\modelSmall{}, $n\approx120$--$160$), with
the clean neutral controls staying $\approx 0$ throughout:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Injection position:} holds at conclusion / opening / middle / random (intersection gap
        $0.71 / 1.20 / 0.48 / 0.49$). Nuance --- the \emph{main effect} flips sign by position: positive at
        conclusion/middle/random but strongly \emph{negative} at the opening ($\approx-1.0$): the RM penalises
        an essay that \emph{opens} with self-positioning, yet still prefers the marginalised author there.
  \item \textbf{Corpus:} replicates on ASAP (sex $0.25$, race $0.33$, class $0.47$, origin $0.49$,
        intersection $0.93$; controls $\le 0.09$).
  \item \textbf{Paraphrase:} holds across three sentence wordings (intersection $0.71 / 0.53 / 0.47$) --- not
        tied to one phrasing.
  \item \textbf{Stance (endorse vs neutral):} PERSUADE essays are one-sided, so a ``disagree'' standpoint is
        incoherent (would be penalised for self-contradiction, an EDA-confirmed confound). Instead we add a
        \emph{neutral} non-committal standpoint --- identical identity grounding, but the closing clause
        neither endorses nor rejects. The gap \emph{persists} under neutral (intersection $0.70\to0.74$, sex
        $0.20\to0.25$; controls $\approx 0$) $\Rightarrow$ the effect is \textbf{standpoint-driven, not about
        rewarding a marginalised author who confidently \emph{agrees}}. (Side note: the humble neutral framing
        raises the \emph{identity-independent} main effect above the confident endorsement.)
\end{itemize}
Tooling: \texttt{run\_positioned\_maineffect.py} gained \texttt{--positions} / \texttt{--paraphrase} /
\texttt{--stance}; \texttt{positionality.py} has paraphrase pools $+$ a neutral-stance variant. Deferred:
referee framing; a topic-natural social-issue corpus; 8B RM.

                  \item \textbf{Economic proxy $=$ the school's free/reduced-price-lunch share} (``most''
                        vs ``few'' students qualify), the standard US school-poverty measure, phrased
                        without a negation. It is school-level, so the proxy measures school poverty
                        where the explicit clause states household income; labels
                        \texttt{high\_poverty\_school}/\texttt{low\_poverty\_school} say so.
                  \item Worst-case clause length delta is \textbf{5 characters} (proxy ethnicity; the
                        composed corner is 4), so every pair passes the Tier-1 gate at the default
                        bounds --- no relaxed intersection threshold, unlike credit and hiring, and 0 of
                        1{,}200 blocks dropped in a 300-essay run.
                  \item \textbf{Sign bug found by the port.} Education's \texttt{ethnicity} was declared
                        \texttt{pole="b"} in the decision-response frame, because the old single-axis
                        marker had white as pole A. Under the factorial that pointed the decision arm at
                        the \emph{reference} pole, i.e.\ it would have measured the discriminatory verdict
                        against a white student and reported it as the protected-pole result. Fixed and
                        pinned by a regression test.
                  \item Real writer attributes (sex, race/ethnicity, grade, ELL, economic and disability
                        status) go into \texttt{cells.jsonl} as \emph{covariates}, not factors: they play
                        no part in detection or debiasing (the matched pair controls them by
                        construction) and belong to the validity section. Their one load-bearing use is
                        the RQ4 check --- after nulling, is the quality signal degraded together with the
                        real-group signal? --- since in this corpus quality is badly entangled with
                        group (ELL 1.78 vs 2.64; economically disadvantaged 2.06 vs 3.15).
                \end{itemize}
          \item \textbf{The stage axis (\texttt{grade\_level}) was widened to 6th grade vs doctoral
                candidate}, and the intermediate rungs (12th grade, final-year bachelor's, master's,
                doctorate) run as a separate \emph{monotonicity sweep}: every rung is contrasted against
                the same 6th-grade reference clause, so one battery run over
                \texttt{stage\_grade12,\dots,stage\_doctorate} traces a curve. A smooth monotone curve
                reads as norm-referenced grading; a jump at a status boundary reads as a prior about the
                writer. The top rung duplicates \texttt{grade\_level} as a consistency check.
                \begin{itemize}
                  \item \textbf{Plausibility is mostly a prompt property, not a text property.} The six
                        PERSUADE prompts that ask pupils about their own schooling draw first-person
                        pupil talk in 46--67\% of essays; the source-text prompts draw it in 0--10\%
                        (``The Face on Mars'' 0.0\%). So the filter selects the nine stage-neutral
                        prompts first, then applies cue rules (own school life, a named stage, own grade,
                        pupil voice, school routine, ``Dear Principal'' letters) as a safety net:
                        6{,}404 essays $\to$ \textbf{3{,}648}, of which 2{,}459 go to prompt selection.
                        ``college''/``university'' mentions are deliberately not a cue.
                  \item \textbf{The low pole is the corpus's own floor} (PERSUADE has no grade below 6),
                        so some essays genuinely are that young \outdated{not after the within-prompt
                        balancing: 8 real 6th-graders remain in the pool; superseded 2026-09-23 by a
                        curriculum justification, see the audit entry}. What no filter can fix is \emph{register}:
                        the essays read like grades 6--10 whatever the marker claims.
                  \item \textbf{Grading is norm-referenced by nature}, and widening the range maximises
                        that: told the writer is in 6th grade, a competent grader should score a fixed
                        essay \emph{higher}, which points opposite to the bias hypothesis. Read the stage
                        gap together with the quality interaction --- norm-referencing predicts a gap that
                        grows with essay quality, a flat offset is a prior about the person. Whether to
                        pin the rubric in the assessment prompt (``against a fixed standard, regardless of
                        who wrote it'') or to run both framings as a factor is still open.
                  \item The proxy clauses are stage-only (``is enrolled in \dots''), never an achievement
                        or duty: ``teaches an undergraduate section'' would be an accomplishment cue, not
                        an age cue --- the mistake the hiring family proxy made with a career gap. All
                        rungs sit within 2 characters of the reference clause, so the Tier-1 gate passes
                        with no relaxation (0.00 discard on every cell).
                  \item \textbf{To watch:} the neutral prompts are graded harder --- only 913 of the 3{,}648
                        are ``strong'' (25\%, vs 40\% before), so the \crossinf{} quality balance is
                        thinner; and they cover grades 6--10 only, with 10 the largest (1{,}708) and 6 the
                        smallest (364), which is the pair for the real-grade confound check.

          \item \textbf{The grader now sees the assignment.} The header used to be
                ``Student essay submission.'' plus the body, so the RM graded an answer to a question it
                could not see --- and the stage-neutral prompts are mostly \emph{text-dependent}: the
                essay argues about a source article, and 977 of the 3{,}647 kept essays (27\%) say ``the
                article''/``the author'' outright (``Exploring Venus'' 74\%). The task text is constant
                per prompt (199--598 characters against a median essay of 2{,}135) and identical across an
                A/B pair, so it is rendered into the header exactly as the hiring header states the target
                role, and omitted where the corpus has none (ASAP). This does not touch the bias contrast;
                it repairs the \emph{quality} arm, since ``does it address the prompt'' is a rubric
                dimension the model could not apply --- and education is the domain \crossinf{} was
                supposed to be interpretable in.
          \item \textbf{Paragraph structure is no longer flattened.} 15{,}004 of the 15{,}594 essays are
                paragraphed (median 4 blank-line breaks) and the cleaner collapsed all of it into one
                line. Blank lines now survive; everything else still collapses to a single space, so a
                hard-wrapped corpus would not gain false breaks (PERSUADE has no lone newlines). Same

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- sample sizes, the embedding cache, and two blocking bugs}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{How many pairs.} Agreed procedure: \emph{pilot, then freeze}. The unit of sample size is the
        record, not the pair (a factorial record gives 8 correlated pairs per axis), so intervals are
        cluster-bootstrapped over records. Probe and eval budgets have different criteria: the probe until
        the direction is stable (split-half cosine, nulled metric flat as the probe grows), the eval until the
        interval supports the claim. A pilot (Qwen3-0.6B + one 8B, full credit and education pools, about
        4{,}000 hiring bios) measures throughput, per-record SDs and the probe-size curve; $n$ is then fixed in
        the configs before the main runs. \texttt{auto\_influence} has a noise floor under \emph{no} effect
        (expected 0.056 at $n{=}200$ records, and up to 0.14 within sampling error), so a removed bias is
        judged by \texttt{abs\_mean\_gap} with its interval, never by \texttt{auto\_influence} alone.
        Data-bound: credit cross-influence (226 matched pairs, $\pm0.065$) and education cross-influence
        (154, $\pm0.079$). \emph{Postponed:} which headline family to pre-register for multiplicity correction.
  \item \textbf{Embedding cache} (\texttt{probes/embedding\_cache.py}). Each unique text is embedded once per
        model; probes, nulling, the $\alpha$-sweep and offline analyses reuse the stored pooled state, and the
        score head is saved beside it, so the pilot's probe-size, seed and bootstrap studies need no GPU. It
        hooks into \texttt{get\_embeddings}, so every runner uses it unchanged. Keyed by text and
        \texttt{max\_length} within a directory fingerprinted by the model (path, revision, config, dtype,
        tokenizer, padding side, head-weight hash); append-only shards, safe for concurrent jobs on PFSS.
        Verified on the real Qwen3-0.6B: a second pass runs no forward pass, offline rewards equal online ones
        bit for bit, and with the same batching cached equals uncached bit for bit. Removes two costs: each
        unique text was embedded $\approx$3.25$\times$ per model (the battery embeds each axis's pairs
        separately, but they share the 8 cells), and the $\alpha$-sweep's fast path was dead since MLX was
        removed, so every swept cell re-ran the model 5 more times.
  \item \textbf{Nondeterminism observed.} On the Mac CPU (bf16) the same model occasionally returned hidden
        states differing by one bf16 rounding step in a few elements for identical input, seen twice and not
        reproducible on demand, and also without the cache. The cache freezes the first computed value, which
        makes reuse exactly reproducible. CUDA has an analogous effect; consistent with the bf16 note in the
        repo's notes.
  \item \textbf{Blocking bug 1 --- no runner could load a model.} \texttt{scoring/backend.py} declared
        \texttt{create\_backend} but no longer defined it (deleted with the MLX backend), while
        \texttt{load\_model} imports it: every runner failed with an \texttt{ImportError}. Restored (one-line
        delegation), with a test; nothing had loaded a model in the test suite.
  \item \textbf{Blocking bug 2 --- DeBERTa was silently mis-scored.} Every reward here is the score head on the
        pooled \emph{last}-token state (so the probe can be projected out just before the head). DeBERTa scores
        the \emph{first} token through a \texttt{ContextPooler} (dense $+$ activation), so for the
        OpenAssistant DeBERTa RM the pipeline's baseline and nulled ``rewards'' were unrelated to the model's
        scores (tiny random DeBERTa: pipeline $0.087, 0.032, \dots$ vs model $-0.002, 0.001, \dots$). Now
        \emph{every} model is checked at load (\texttt{verify\_score\_path}: the pipeline reward must reproduce
        the model's own score on two texts) and a mismatch is refused. Qwen3-0.6B passes with difference
        0.0000. \textbf{Open:} DeBERTa needs its own pooling and projection site (the nonlinearity sits between
        the token state and the head), and the QRM-Gemma quantile head (remote code) is untested --- both are
        blocked until then, which affects the eleven-RM set.
  \item \textbf{DeBERTa will most likely be removed from the RM list} (decision noted, not yet executed). It is a
        2023 encoder RM, as far as we know no longer used in current RLHF, and near the bottom of RewardBench. It
        was kept for one reason: to vary the RM \emph{type} for RQ6. Against it: the null-space method needs a
        linear head on the projected state, and DeBERTa's first-token \texttt{ContextPooler} puts a nonlinearity in
        between (projecting the pooler output would work, but at a different representational level from every
        other model, so the complexity comparison is not like-for-like); it was trained on inputs of at most 512
        tokens, so the education essays ($\approx$750--900 tokens with header) do not fit and the A2
        conclusion-position sentence would be lost; a weak RM leaves \texttt{acc\_baseline} near chance, so
        cross-influence is uninterpretable. \textbf{QRM-Gemma-2-27B replaces its role, and better:} it pools the last
        token like the pipeline, and its score is linear in that state for a fixed gate (a softmax gating network
        weights five quantile-regression heads). Its backbone is Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B-v0.2, so pinning the
        Skywork Gemma entry to v0.2 gives a same-backbone scalar-vs-distributional pair (and, if the backbone was
        frozen, identical representations). Needed before it runs: a small adapter for its gated head. On the gate:
        it reads the prompt/response boundary, so it sees the user turn only. In the direct-scoring arms the marker
        is in the response, so the gate never sees it; in the decision-response arm the marker is in the user turn,
        so the gate does see it --- but every comparison that arm makes (fair vs discriminatory verdict) is between
        two texts with the \emph{same} user turn, so the gate is identical on both sides and cancels. It would only
        matter for a comparison across user turns with different markers (e.g.\ win rates for women vs men),
        which no current arm makes, and for claims about \emph{where} the demographic information lives (RQ3). To do if confirmed: drop the table row, ``eleven'' becomes ``ten'', and
        rephrase RQ6's RM-type sentence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- methodology decision: the paper rests on a cross-marker decision design}
\textbf{Why.} In the direct-scoring arms the user asks for an assessment and the ``assistant'' merely recites
the application, essay or bio, so the RM grades an off-task answer. Worse, the marker is then something the
\emph{assistant} says (``the applicant is Black''), and a safety-trained RM may react to the assistant
verbalising a protected attribute --- possibly differently per value --- rather than to the person. The A/B
design cancels a generic ``mentions a demographic'' effect but not a value-specific one. Earlier results fit a
generic sensitivity (auto-influence saturated on nearly every axis, the A2 controls reacted, the essay effect
was a symmetric main effect). The decision-response arm has the realistic structure (document in the user
turn, the policy's decision scored), but its discriminatory verdict states the protected reason openly, which
is exactly what safety training punishes, and it only ever shows the protected pole, so it cannot detect
disparate treatment --- the core high-risk harm.

\textbf{Decided (four points):}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Primary evidence: a cross-marker decision design} (to build). The harm claims (RQ1's behavioural
        part, RQ2) rest on it. For one strong record, the 8 factorial cells go into the \emph{user} turn; the
        responses do not name the attribute, so the identical text is valid under every cell: approve (merit
        reasons), decline (a plausible neutral reason), evasive; the blatant verdict stays as a separate item.
        Per cell $D = r(\text{approve}) - r(\text{decline})$; the disparity is $D(\text{protected}) -
        D(\text{reference})$ per axis, averaged over the other factors --- how much more the RM favours declining,
        e.g., her than him with the identical profile, i.e.\ what a policy trained or selected against this RM would
        learn. Intersection and additivity carry over to $D$. Size: 8 prompts $\times$ 4 responses $=$ 32 texts per
        record and template, as in the direct arm; about 19{,}000 texts per domain and model at 300 records $\times$ 2
        templates (estimate: $\sim$10\,min on an 8B, $\sim$1.5\,h on a 70B for credit; about $3\times$ for education).
        Every text is unique, so the embedding cache does not shrink the first pass but makes all later probe,
        $\alpha$, bootstrap and seed work free. QRM's gate sees the prompt, and here the prompts differ, so its
        nonlinear second pathway is in play in this design.
  \item \textbf{Direct scoring becomes the mechanism layer.} Kept for RQ3 (LEACE $+$ non-linear probe), RQ4's
        mechanics (does projection remove the direction?) and RQ5 transfer (does a direction fitted with the marker
        in the \emph{response} remove the disparity when it is in the \emph{prompt}?). Its byte-exact single-slot pairs
        give the sharpest directions. Claims are narrowed to ``the reward is sensitive to protected attributes under
        controlled substitution'' --- never ``the RM assesses applicants in a biased way''.
  \item \textbf{A2 standpoint is kept, scoped to grading}: a student's own standpoint inside an essay submitted for
        grading is the one direct-format placement with a natural deployment analogue (automated grading).
  \item \textbf{Cross-influence is dropped in its direct form and redone in the decision format.} Its premise, that
        the RM judges applicant quality in an off-task recitation, fails (and after length matching we expect
        \texttt{acc\_baseline} near chance). Redone as Kumar et al.'s accuracy deviation in a realistic placement:
        for strong \emph{and} weak applicants (the decision arm currently uses strong ones only), the correct decision
        (approve strong, decline weak) against the incorrect one, $\text{acc} = P(r(\text{correct}) >
        r(\text{incorrect}))$, with the marker in the user turn; cross-influence is the change in acc between
        protected and reference cells. Caveat for credit: the label is repayment among granted loans and several
        fields run against common sense, so ``correct'' can look wrong to a sensible RM --- report the
        common-sense-scorer reference with it.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Empirical check on the direct arm:} once the cross-marker design exists, measure the same factorial with
the marker in the response and in the prompt. If the direct-arm effects largely vanish under prompt placement,
they were placement artefacts and direct scoring stays purely mechanism.

\textbf{Literature check on the placement} (does RM-bias work put the attribute in the assistant turn?):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \citet{kumar2025prefix} --- the source of auto- and cross-influence --- \emph{does} put it in the response:
        they prepend ``I am a woman.''/``I am a man.'' to responses (verified in their Section~4), on Stanford Human
        Preferences and Anthropic-HH. But there the responses are human-written answers (Reddit, for SHP), so the
        prefix is the \emph{answerer} identifying themselves, and the question is whether the RM rewards answers
        by one group more. Our direct arm puts a third-person description of the \emph{person being judged} into the
        response; I found no precedent for that, so it extends their placement rather than replicating it.
  \item \citet{song2025groupfairness} explicitly criticise counterfactual attribute swapping: it assumes users of
        different groups ask identical questions, and that users state their group in the prompt, which they
        rarely do. For high-risk decisions the second point largely does not apply to us --- the attribute belongs
        to the \emph{subject} of the document, and CVs, loan files and submissions routinely carry names, ages and
        family details --- and the proxy encodings (names, school poverty) are exactly that form.
  \item Other recent RM work places demographic information in the prompt: persona steering prompts
        (``Reward Model Perspectives'', arXiv:2510.06391, 2025), and text-to-image RM bias via prompt and image
        (Abdel Magid et al., arXiv:2604.13305, 2026). The base paper \citep{fein2026onebias} studies response
        properties (length, overconfidence, style, answer order) and no demographic attribute.
  \item LLM audit studies of decisions put the attribute in the \emph{input} and judge the model's output (e.g.\ the
        name-based audit of \citet{haim2024whatsinaname}) --- the convention the cross-marker decision design
        follows.
\end{itemize}
So the restructuring aligns the primary evidence with the established placement for decision audits, and keeps
Kumar et al.'s metrics while fixing where their premise did not transfer.

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- cross-marker build, stage 1: items and responses}
\textbf{Built} (\texttt{pairs/cross\_marker.py}, no model yet). Items are read from the generators'
\texttt{cells.jsonl}, so they passed the Tier-1 gate, carry the seeded proxy names and are byte-identical to
the cells the direct arm's pairs are cut from (the placement check needs exactly that). Per block: the 8 cells
plus an unmarked control (the cell text with its clause removed; all 8 must agree) $\times$ 4 responses
$=$ 36 texts. Four response types, none naming an attribute value or category (enforced by a test against a
per-domain lexicon of every word that varies between the design's clauses, all proxy names and category words):
\emph{approve} (merit reasons), \emph{decline} (neutral reason), \emph{coded} (a stereotype-congruent reason
that names nothing), \emph{evasive}. Each type has 3 parallel paraphrases; one is drawn per record and used for
all its cells, templates and encodings, so within-record contrasts differ only by the marker. Currently one
coded reason per domain: credit ``income may not stay stable'', hiring ``availability in the coming months'',
education ``not confident it is entirely the student's own work''. (2026-09-27: all responses rewritten to one
token length, same reasons; see the \texttt{cross\_marker.py} review entry.)

\textbf{Open: more coded reasons.} One coded reason per domain fits some axes' stereotypes and not others ---
income stability fits the young and the married applicant, hardly sex on its own --- so a null coded disparity
on an axis may only mean the excuse does not fit that axis's stereotype. Options:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{One congruent reason per axis}, e.g.\ credit: a credit history too short to judge (age),
        household obligations that may grow during the term (marital status), earnings that may be too low for
        the commitment (sex); hiring: availability (family, current), a lack of assertiveness for the role (sex),
        a lack of seniority or a likely early move (age); education: authorship doubt (current), language short
        of standard academic English (ethnicity), an argument driven by feeling rather than analysis (sex),
        limited preparation (economic status). Drafts only.
  \item \emph{A reference-congruent (counter-stereotypical) reason as control}, e.g.\ ``may struggle to adapt to
        new systems'' for age: if the RM accepts stereotype-congruent excuses more readily for the protected
        group, the congruent reason's gain must exceed the incongruent one's --- a stereotype-congruence
        $\times$ group interaction inside the same design.
  \item \emph{Cost}: each extra reason adds 9 texts per block (+25\,\%). Cost-neutral alternative: draw one
        coded reason per record from the pool (seeded), so per-reason $n$ drops to $n/k$. Decide after the
        pilot, whose per-record SD shows whether $n/k$ is enough.
\end{itemize}
Constraint for every coded reason: it must be valid under every cell, i.e.\ a claim about the record, never about
the group (``close to retirement'' is out --- it asserts an age, false for the 30-year-old cells).

\textbf{The blatant verdict is the floor.} The old decision arm (\texttt{run\_decision\_response.py}, kept as a
separate item) scores a decline that states the protected reason openly --- the form safety training punishes
most reliably. It is the floor an RM must clear: rewarding it is the strongest possible finding, while punishing
it says nothing about the silent forms the cross-marker design measures. It can also be measured
\emph{cross-marker}: a category-naming decline (``given the applicant's personal circumstances, I would not
recommend \dots'') names the category but not the value, so it fits every cell, and its disparity asks whether
the RM tolerates an openly attribute-based decline more when the applicant is a woman than a man. (A
value-naming variant, whose verdict names the cell's own value, is also a valid difference-in-differences, but
its text then changes with the cell.) Not built; it would cost the same as another response type.

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- cross-marker build, stage 2: metrics}
\texttt{scoring/cross\_marker\_metrics.py} (pure; tested on planted effects). The record is the unit: rewards are
averaged over a record's templates, every contrast is taken within the record, and CIs are a percentile
bootstrap over records, reported with the paired effect $d_z$. Margins per cell: $D$ (approve $-$ decline, the
headline), $D_\text{coded}$, $E$ (coded $-$ decline: does the stereotype-congruent excuse gain more for the
protected pole?) and $V$ (evasive $-$ approve). Disparity = mean over the other factors' settings of
$M(\text{pole A}) - M(\text{pole B})$; \textbf{a negative $D$-disparity means the RM favours declining the
protected pole more}. The full $2^3$ decomposition on the DiD scale: main effects, three two-way interactions
(the DiD of a pair, averaged over the third factor), the three-way term; the additivity gap (corner $-$ $\Sigma$
mains) equals the three-way term$/4$, since two-way interactions cancel in the corner. Decision accuracy: correct
$=$ approve for strong, the neutral decline for weak records; cross-influence $=$ acc(pole B) $-$ acc(pole A),
strong, weak and balanced; plus the unmarked control and the AUC of $D$ between strong and weak records (a
constant approve or length preference scores 0.5 on balanced accuracy and AUC). Credit's marital status under
the proxy encoding stays explicit, so it gets no single-axis disparity there (as in the direct manifests).

\textbf{Credit reference} (\texttt{substrates/credit\_reference.py}): every rendered field scored in a lender's
intuitive direction, fixed a priori (not ordered by this dataset's good rates), equal weights; duration and
amount by rank. AUC against \texttt{credit\_good} on the 803 kept records: \textbf{0.683} (the audit's version:
$\approx$0.69; a data-fitted model: $\approx$0.79). The RM's AUC of $D$ is to be read against it.

\textbf{Revised the same day} (after review): (1) \emph{accuracy and AUC are decided per template}, then averaged
within the record (which stays the bootstrap unit). Deciding on the template-averaged $D$ made both drift towards
0 or 1 as templates are added --- $P(\text{correct}) = \Phi(\mu\sqrt{k}/\sigma)$ for $k$ averaged templates ---
whereas one decision per template estimates $\Phi(\mu/\sigma)$ whatever $k$ is, and matches deployment (the RM
scores one concrete prompt). AUC: per (template, cell), then averaged. The linear statistics (disparities,
interactions, levels) still average templates first, which changes nothing but noise. (2) \emph{A second
scale-free effect}: besides $d_z$ (mean$/$SD of the per-record contrast --- a consistency measure that shrinks
when records differ, even for a large effect), every disparity, interaction and additivity gap is reported in
units of the SD across the group's records of $D$ on the unmarked control: the effect as a fraction of how much
this RM's decision margin varies between applicants anyway, comparable across RMs with different reward
scales. Its CI re-estimates that SD in every bootstrap replicate. Kept as they were: the stratified bootstrap of
balanced accuracy (strong and weak group sizes are fixed by design), no proxy marital disparity for credit, and
$d_z$ undefined when all records share one value.

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- cross-marker build, stage 3: runner}
\texttt{runners/run\_cross\_marker.py}, configs \texttt{demographic\_\{credit,cv,edu\}\_crossmarker\_*\_qwen06.yaml}
(settings under \texttt{extra.cross\_marker}; CLI overrides). Items come from \texttt{cells.jsonl} next to the
config's direct manifest; after regenerating credit, 10{,}096 sampled pair texts were checked equal to their cells
(byte-identical, as the placement check needs). The direct arm's direction for every (encoding, axis) the
factorial has pairs for is fitted and projected out (RQ5 transfer), and \emph{every record in any of their probe
splits is excluded} from evaluation (new accessor \texttt{probe\_record\_ids()}, so the grouped split is reused,
not reimplemented). Records are drawn seeded per class; one is taken only if it has every requested (encoding,
template) block and \emph{all} its texts fit \texttt{max\_length} untruncated --- right truncation would cut the
response, which carries the decision. One embedding pass per encoding; each reward column (baseline,
\texttt{null\_direct:<axis>}) is the score head on those states. Outputs: a summary JSON (settings, selection,
probe metadata, metrics per encoding and column, credit reference) and a per-text reward JSONL (ids and rewards,
no text), from which every statistic can be recomputed offline.

\textbf{Smoke run} (Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-0.6B on the Mac GPU, credit, explicit, 6 strong $+$ 6 weak records ---
a plumbing check, \emph{no result}): 24 blocks $\times$ 36 texts, no length drops; 150 probe records excluded,
leaving 463 strong and \textbf{190 weak} records available (weak is credit's binding pool); an identical rerun did
0 forward passes (7.6\,s) and reproduced every reward and metric exactly. CPU was too slow for even a smoke run
(11\,s per batch of 8). The MPS states went to a separate cache directory: the cache fingerprint does not include
the device. \textbf{Observation that matters for the design:} this RM prefers the neutral \emph{decline} over
\emph{approve} for strong and weak records alike (by 0.7--2.3 reward depending on the paraphrase; the evasive
answer sits near $-4.5$; 2026-09-27: the neutral decline was then the longer response at every paraphrase, by
2--7 words, so part of this preference may be length --- re-check on the rebalanced pools). The DiD cancels such a wording-level preference, but it pins strong-record accuracy at 0,
so accuracy-based cross-influence cannot move on strong records --- a floor effect. The threshold-free AUC of $D$ is
unaffected by a constant shift, so a threshold-free cross-influence (AUC in reference cells $-$ AUC in protected
cells) would be the robust version. Built in stage 4.

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- cross-marker build, stage 4: mechanism layer}
\textbf{Threshold-free cross-influence}: AUC of $D$ (strong vs weak records) in pole-B cells minus pole-A cells, per
template and axis pair; CI from resampling strong and weak records separately with the same draws for every cell.

\textbf{Fifth response, \emph{overt}}: a decline that names the protected attributes as \emph{categories} (``taking
the applicant's sex, age and marital status into account, I would not recommend \dots''), never their values, so
it fits every cell. The lexicon test now separates value words (banned in every response) from category words
(allowed only in \emph{overt}). Margins $O = r(\text{overt}) - r(\text{decline})$ (the penalty for naming the
categories; a positive $O$-disparity means the overt decline is tolerated more for the protected pole --- the
cross-marker form of the blatant verdict) and $D_\text{overt}$. 45 texts per block.

\textbf{Directions and the exact decomposition.} With $r = w\cdot h + b$, projecting out a unit direction $u$ changes
the decision disparity by exactly $-(w\cdot u)(\Delta_\text{int}\cdot u)$, where $\Delta_\text{int}$ is the marker
$\times$ decision interaction of the states: a direction removes the disparity only if the head reads it
\emph{and} it carries the interaction. Directions: the direct arm's per axis and their joint span (RQ5 transfer);
fitted on this design's states, \emph{prompt} (per axis; same response, marker in the prompt), \emph{interaction}
(per axis; $\Delta_\text{int}$) and \emph{unfair} (overt $-$ neutral decline, same prompt: the RM's representation of
an openly discriminatory decision). The latter three are \textbf{cross-fitted} (5 quality-stratified record folds;
each fold nulled with the direction fitted on the others, so in-sample nulling --- which removes the mean disparity
by construction --- never enters a number). Reported per encoding: the cross-nulling matrix (every direction
projected out, every metric recomputed), cosines between all directions against split-half reliability ceilings,
head alignment $\cos(w,u)$, each direction's share of each axis's disparity, an $\alpha$-sweep of the own-axis
directions, and the \textbf{placement check} (the same records' byte-identical cells with the marker in the
response: direct gap vs prompt main effect vs DiD, in one unit). A degenerate (zero) direction gets undefined
geometry, not a cosine of 0.

\textbf{Smoke run} (Qwen3-0.6B, credit, explicit, 10 strong $+$ 10 weak; plumbing, \emph{no result}): $w\cdot
\Delta_\text{int}$ from the states reproduces the reward-based $D$-disparity to 3 decimals (bf16 rounding); a
cached rerun took 12\,s with 0 misses. Split-half reliability at $n=20$: direct 0.97--0.99, prompt 0.94--0.98,
interaction 0.50--0.75 (second differences are noisier, as expected --- the pilot sizes $n$ for them). Patterns to
check in the pilot, none of them claims at this $n$: (i) the held-out \emph{unfair} direction removed most of the
age, marital and intersection disparities, the own-axis direct directions little (their joint span more); (ii)
sex showed the placement-artefact pattern --- a large direct gap (marker in the response, $+0.44$ in scaled units)
with prompt main effect and DiD $\approx 0$; (iii) the overt decline was penalised less for women ($O$-disparity
$+0.33$, intersection $+0.54$) against an overall penalty of $-2.55$; (iv) the AUC of $D$ was below 0.5 in both
smoke samples (0.42 here) against 0.67 for the common-sense scorer on the same records --- if it holds, this RM's
decision margin runs against the credit labels.

\subsection*{2026-09-24 --- audit items 4.6 and 5.4: an analysis rule for comparisons between real groups}
The real attributes kept on the record (PERSUADE writer sex, race/ethnicity, ELL and economic status; Bias-in-Bios
gender; credit's real marital status) are confounded with what the model judges. In the current PERSUADE pool
(1{,}364 essays, balanced strong/weak within prompt) 73\% of the Black writers wrote weak essays and only 103 are
ELL writers; in the hiring pool a single profession can be 88--94\% one gender. Any comparison between real
groups --- a reward level or a within-record quantity such as an injected marker's effect --- follows one rule:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item strata of quality label $\times$ document-length tercile;
  \item within each stratum, the prompt (education) or profession (hiring) as a fixed effect;
  \item only strata with $\ge30$ records per group, pooled by stratum size, with the coverage reported; coverage
        below 0.5 $\Rightarrow$ n/a;
  \item a bootstrap over records for the interval;
  \item the estimate without the fixed effect reported alongside (sensitivity).
\end{enumerate}
Unconditional contrasts between real groups are not reported. \emph{Why a fixed effect and not a third stratum:}
measured on the current pools, adding the prompt or profession as a stratum leaves almost nothing usable
(PERSUADE sex 0.19, race 0.00, economic 0.20, ELL 0.00; Bias-in-Bios gender 0.08 of the records), whereas quality
$\times$ length alone covers PERSUADE sex 0.99, White vs Hispanic/Latino 0.99, Black vs white 0.79, economic status
1.00, ELL 0.49 (so ELL comparisons are n/a), and Bias-in-Bios gender 1.00. The credit real-field cross-check meets
the rule by exact matching (item 4.2). Nothing implements the rule yet --- no real-group analysis exists in the
code; the first one gets it as a shared function with tests. These are validity checks, secondary to the
pre-registered headline family. \textbf{This closes audit items 4 and 5.}

\paragraph{Rule for \texttt{probe\_records} (fixed before the pilot).} For every direct direction used for nulling
(each axis and the intersection, explicit and proxy), fit it on the first $N$ probe records for
$N \in \{25, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 300\}$. The probe sets are nested in $N$ (stratified by quality), and every $N$
is evaluated on one fixed eval set: the records outside the largest probe set (up to 2{,}000 pairs, about one per
record). A point passes when (a) its split-half cosine is at least 0.90 --- conservative, since it is the
reliability of halves of $N/2$ records --- and (b) its nulled $|$gap$|$ on the eval set, averaged per record, lies
inside the 95\% record-bootstrap interval of the value at $N=300$. A direction's answer is the smallest $N$ from
which every larger point passes too (one lucky point at small $N$ does not decide). A domain's
\texttt{probe\_records} is the largest answer over its directions, encodings and both models. A direction that
fails even at 300 is reported, and the domain keeps 300.

\paragraph{Rule for $n$, records per group (fixed before the pilot).} For each headline contrast of the decision
margin $D$ --- every axis's disparity, the intersection, the additivity gap --- and separately the two-way
interactions, the pilot gives the per-record SD in scaled units, $r = \mathrm{sd}/\mathrm{scale\_sd}$
(\texttt{scale\_sd} = the SD across records of $D$ on the unmarked control). The records needed to detect a scaled
effect $\delta$ with 80\% power at two-sided $\alpha/m$ are
$n = \lceil ((z_{1-\alpha/(2m)} + z_{0.80})\, r/\delta)^2 \rceil$, for the largest $r$ among the contrasts, capped
by the records available (credit weak: 194). $\delta$ (the smallest scaled effect of interest) and $m$ (the Holm
family size) are fixed together with the headline family, which is still open; the sizing tool therefore reports
$\delta \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$ $\times$ $m \in \{1, 4, 12\}$. The rule reads only SDs, never an effect's mean
or interval (the tool is blinded to them and a test pins that), so $n$ cannot follow the pilot's effects. The record selection
is seeded, so the main runs' records are a prefix of the pilot's only when \texttt{probe\_records} is the same in
both: a different value excludes other probe records, which changes the remaining pool and so the seeded order.
(Corrected 2026-09-26; the earlier sentence claimed a prefix unconditionally.)

\subsection*{2026-09-26 --- the QRM adapter}
The log of 2026-09-24 left QRM-Gemma-2-27B blocked (``a small adapter for its gated head''). Done, tested on a
tiny random QRM; the real checkpoint is first loaded on the cluster.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The remote code does not import.} The checkpoint's \texttt{modeling\_custom.py} imports
        \texttt{LLAMA\_INPUTS\_DOCSTRING}, which transformers no longer has (neither the cluster's 4.57.6 nor 5.17);
        it was dead code there, but the import fails first. We therefore carry our own implementation of the
        architecture (\texttt{scoring/qrm.py}), with the checkpoint's parameter names (checked against its weight
        index), loaded by the backend when a checkpoint declares \texttt{Gemma2ForQuantileSequenceClassification}. No
        remote code runs.
  \item \textbf{The score, as read from the reference code.} Five HelpSteer2 objectives $\times$ 19 quantiles, all
        linear in the last-token state $h$; a gate (MLP $+$ softmax, times a learned scale) over the objectives, read
        from the state at the end of the \emph{user} turn; score $=\sum_k g_k \cdot \mathrm{mean}_q (Rh)_{k,q}$. The gate
        depends on the prompt only (causal attention), so it is identical for all responses to one prompt --- which
        confirms the 2026-09-24 argument that it cancels in the decision arm's same-prompt comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Projection site.} Unchanged: $h$ is projected, the gate is computed on the unprojected pass and held
        fixed; for a fixed gate the score is linear in $h$ with the text's effective head $g\,\bar R$. A per-text
        gate is new to the pipeline: it is computed in the same forward pass, cached beside the states (a
        \texttt{gates/} sub-cache) and passed wherever rewards are computed from states. Linear-head models are
        untouched, down to their cache fingerprint (the pilot's cached states stay valid).
  \item \textbf{The gate pathway, measured.} In the cross-marker design the marker is in the prompt, so the gate
        sees it: a disparity can run through the gate, which no last-token projection removes. For a gated model
        the runner adds a \texttt{gate\_fixed} column --- every row rescored with the gate of its record's unmarked
        prompt --- whose disparities run through $h$ alone; baseline $-$ \texttt{gate\_fixed} is the gate's part. This
        is the RQ3 question ``where does the demographic information live'' for QRM. The geometry uses the mean
        effective head, so its \texttt{did\_from\_states} is approximate for QRM.
  \item \textbf{Checked at load.} \texttt{verify\_score\_path} now scores chat-formatted conversations (as every
        pipeline text is; QRM needs the user/assistant boundary) and, for a gated head, also compares the
        pipeline's gates with the model's.
  \item \textbf{QRM's backbone is bit-identical to Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B-v0.2} (checked 2026-09-26 on the
        Hub: QRM revision \texttt{83d5ac5}, Skywork v0.2 revision \texttt{a92f2ec}). The QRM paper says the backbone
        stays frozen in both training stages, and the model card names v0.2 as its backbone; the files confirm it.

\subsection*{2026-09-26 --- design: a comparative (two-applicant) arm}
\emph{Design only; to be implemented after the review of \texttt{pairs/} and \texttt{scoring/}, whose factorial
cells, rendering and metrics it reuses.}

\paragraph{Idea.} The user turn presents \emph{two} applications (or bios, or essays) and asks which one should get
the loan / the interview / the award; the response chooses one. The protected attribute enters as a contrast
\emph{between} the two applicants, in the prompt, as it would when a policy model shortlists or ranks. Pairings:
strong vs strong and weak vs weak (does the marker break a tie of merit?), strong vs weak (a correct answer exists:
can the marker overturn a merit-based choice?). More realistic than any current arm --- real decisions compare
different people, never byte-identical twins --- and it adds a third placement of the marker for RQ5.

\paragraph{Control: a counterbalanced within-pair contrast.} Different records do not mean an uncontrolled comparison.
Each record pair $(X, Y)$ is shown under both marker assignments (X protected and Y reference, and swapped) and in
both orders (X first, Y first): 4 prompts per pair. With $M = r(\text{choose }X) - r(\text{choose }Y)$, the marker
effect is $M\,|\,X\text{ protected} \;-\; M\,|\,Y\text{ protected}$, averaged over the two orders. Everything about the
two records --- quality, length, content --- appears on both sides and cancels exactly; the order swap removes position
bias, which reward models and LLM judges are known to have. So the arm keeps a within-pair difference-in-differences,
not only an average over unmatched records.

\paragraph{Design choices (proposed).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Cells}: the two applicants differ in one axis at a time (the others held equal, at each of their
        settings), plus the intersection corner --- the full crossing would be $8\times 8$ cells per pair.
  \item \emph{Responses}: attribute-free, symmetric and length-matched (``Applicant A is the stronger candidate
        \dots''), naming the applicant by position label only; possibly a ``neither'' and an evasive response as in
        the cross-marker set.
  \item \emph{Units}: every record in at most one pair, so the record pair is the bootstrap unit
        (\texttt{scoring/intervals.py} applies unchanged); pairs drawn within quality strata (and, for strong vs
        weak, across them), optionally matched on document length to cut noise.
  \item \emph{Scope}: hiring (shortlisting is the most natural comparative decision) and credit. Education does
        not fit as is: two essays of up to $\approx$1{,}500 tokens exceed \texttt{max\_length} 2{,}048, and grading is
        absolute rather than comparative; only with a comparative framing (a scholarship) and a larger
        \texttt{max\_length} on models that allow it.
  \item \emph{Cost}: 4 prompts per pair $\times$ a few responses, with prompts about twice as long --- per record
        cheaper than the cross-marker design's 45 texts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What it would measure.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The marker effect on the choice margin, per pairing type; tie-breaking (strong--strong, weak--weak) is where
        discrimination is most plausible in practice.
  \item For strong vs weak: how often the marker overturns the merit-correct choice, and an \emph{exchange rate} ---
        the marker effect relative to the quality effect on the same margin (how much merit a marker is worth).
  \item \textbf{Leak-back (RQ5): a transfer matrix.} Three placements of the marker --- in the response (direct
        scoring), in a single application in the prompt (cross-marker), in one of two applications (comparative).
        Fit a direction in each, null it in each other: a $3\times3$ matrix of how much of each disparity the other
        situations' directions remove. That answers ``in which situations does the bias leak back'' for debiasing
        the score but not the model; the comparison ``direct-scoring directions vs decision directions'' is its
        first row and column.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveat, and a question of its own (RQ3).} In the comparative prompt the reward model must bind the attribute
to the right applicant (``the chosen one is the woman''). A single linear direction may not capture that binding, so a
projection can fail there for a structural reason rather than because the bias is ``deeper''. LEACE plus a non-linear
probe on the comparative states would separate the two --- informative either way.

\paragraph{Open decisions.} Confirmatory (e.g.\ as the RQ5 transfer test) or exploratory --- to be settled with the
headline family; the exact response set; whether education is in scope; pair counts, from the re-run pilot.

\subsection*{2026-09-26 --- CPU cost of the cross-marker runs: profiled and removed}
In the pilot the CPU-side phases took 31--69\% of every run, the mechanism layer most of it, and on the 8B the
mechanism layer grew with the hidden size. A \texttt{cProfile} run on the cluster (credit, \modelSmall{},
explicit, every state from the embedding cache) found the cause: \textbf{thread oversubscription}. Torch used every
core of the node for each of $\approx$15{,}000 tiny per-record sums in the direction fitting, and paid the
thread-pool overhead each time: mechanism layer 1{,}289\,s, whole run 1{,}513\,s. With
\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS} capped (2, 8 and 16 all give 257--258\,s; the cap matters, not its value) the same run
took 258\,s. \texttt{cluster/config.yaml} now sets 8 for every task. GPU transfers were not the cause (4\,s).

The remaining CPU work was then rewritten without changing any number, before the code review, so the review sees
the final code:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metrics} (\texttt{scoring/cross\_marker\_metrics.py}): the reward table is an array
        [record $\times$ template $\times$ cell $\times$ response] built from an index computed once per encoding
        (\texttt{RewardIndex}), instead of a dict pass per reward column; the $2^3$ decomposition runs on all
        records at once; the bootstrap draws and the resampled scale SD are computed once per record set; the
        bootstrap AUCs (cross-influence, quality tracking, within length strata) count pairs weighted by each
        replicate's multiplicities instead of ranking every replicate --- exactly the same Mann--Whitney counts.
  \item \textbf{$\alpha$-sweep}: it recomputed the full metrics 60 times to keep one disparity and one AUC per point;
        it now computes those two (\texttt{sweep\_point}).
  \item \textbf{Prepare}: every conversation was rendered twice (length guard, then rows) and tokenized one call
        per text; now rendered once and counted in one call per record (credit: 35\,s $\to$ 11\,s).
  \item \textbf{Direction fitting}: the per-record contrast sums run as a few large indexed sums, independent of
        the thread setting.
\end{itemize}
Local profile, credit with 479 records per encoding: mechanism layer 29\,s $\to$ 4.8\,s, metrics 29\,s $\to$ 3.6\,s,
placement check 4.7\,s $\to$ 0.9\,s. \textbf{Check:} a regression test pins every number the metrics, the placement
check and the mechanism layer produce on fixed synthetic data (three designs, both encodings, ties, a record with a
missing template); after the rewrite all 11{,}950 metric values and 19{,}416 mechanism values are bit-identical to
the code before it.

\emph{Found on the way:} Python's built-in \texttt{sum} over floats has been compensated (Neumaier) since 3.12, plain
left-to-right before. The reference \texttt{factorial\_effects} therefore rounded differently on the cluster
(Python 3.10) than on the Mac (3.14), and an effect that is exactly zero in exact arithmetic came out as
$\pm 10^{-17}$ with a machine-dependent sign --- which \texttt{share\_negative} counts. The vectorised version
always uses the compensated sum, so it is machine-independent. It changes nothing but \texttt{share\_negative} in
such tie cases, and only relative to earlier cluster runs.

\subsection*{2026-09-26 --- Nemotron-70B adapter; tokenizing as each model's authors do}
\textbf{Adapter.} Nemotron-70B-Reward is loaded as a causal LM and wrapped (\texttt{scoring/logit\_reward.py}): the
score head is row 0 of the output matrix (no bias), the pooled state the usual last-token state, so the projection
site and every arm are unchanged. The load-time check compares the pipeline's reward with the model's own logit, and a
test on a tiny model reproduces the model card's \texttt{generate(...).scores[0][0][0]} read-out. Any causal-LM
checkpoint not listed with its token is refused: as a sequence classifier it would get a randomly initialised score
head that nothing downstream would notice.

\textbf{Tokenization.} Building it showed that the pipeline's convention --- format with the chat template, then
tokenize with the tokenizer's defaults, which add a BOS --- differs from the template's own token ids for four
ladder models: Nemotron-70B, both RB2 models and Skywork-Gemma-2-27B (their templates contain no BOS). What matters
is how each model's authors score it: the Skywork cards and RewardBench (the RB2 authors' benchmark) tokenize with
the defaults, so those keep the BOS; Nemotron's card tokenizes the template's ids directly, so it alone is scored
without one (\texttt{TEMPLATE\_TOKENIZED}). The other six models agree with their templates either way. The loader
logs the relation for every model. None of the four had been run, so no result changes.

\subsection*{2026-09-26 --- proxy names: the pools' cohort and class gaps}
Follow-up to the 2026-09-16 assumption that a name's sex signal dominates its age-cohort and class signals
(pairs/ review, \texttt{markers.py}).

\textbf{What would bias a proxy contrast.} Names are drawn uniformly per block, so a single name's cohort or class
signal adds noise, not bias. A proxy contrast is biased by the difference between the two pools' \emph{means},
times the RM's sensitivity to that signal. Two consequences for the planned check: (a) leave-names-out cannot see
this, because every fold carries the same pool imbalance; the pool gap is a property of the names alone and needs
no model run. The sensitivity does need one (below).

\textbf{Data} (\texttt{configs/name\_pool\_signals.yaml}, kept in sync with the pools by a test). Cohort: the
count-weighted median birth year of each name for the pool's sex, SSA national counts 1880--2017 (R package
\texttt{babynames} 1.0.1, CC0). Class: the share of mothers with some college or more among children given the
name, New York birth records 1994--2012 \citep[][supplement, Table~A1]{gaddis2017lakisha} --- an objective SES
correlate of the naming families, not a perceived-class rating. Haim et al.\ drew all 40 names from Gaddis's set,
so every name is covered. \citet{crabtree2023validated} have perceived income and education ratings, but only for
5 of our 40 first names, all in one pool (Lakisha, Latonya, Latoya, Tamika, Tanisha), so they cannot give a gap.

\textbf{Gaps} (pole A pool mean $-$ pole B pool mean; SE between names, 10 per pool; between-name SD within a pool
$\approx$9--13 years and 7--14 points):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
  \toprule
  contrast & used in & cohort (years) & mothers with college (pp) \\
  \midrule
  sex $\mid$ white names   & credit, hiring, education & $-1.0$ (5.5) & $+5.9$ (6.0) \\
  sex $\mid$ Black names   & education                 & $-5.5$ (4.3) & $-2.4$ (5.1) \\
  ethnicity $\mid$ female  & education                 & $-0.7$ (4.7) & $\mathbf{-43.7}$ (6.3) \\
  ethnicity $\mid$ male    & education                 & $+3.8$ (5.1) & $\mathbf{-35.4}$ (4.7) \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proxy sex, credit and hiring: the pools are balanced} on cohort and class (both gaps well inside one
        SE and small against the between-name spread). The pool-level premise of the assumption holds. The
        cohort signal also competes with the birth year stated in the same clause.
  \item \textbf{Proxy sex, education:} as above for white names; among Black names the female pool is $\approx$5 years
        older (Lakisha, Latonya, Tamika: 1976--79; Jayvon, Keyshawn, Latrell: 2002--06). The student clause
        states no age, so a cohort-old name is at most an oddness cue. Modest.
  \item \textbf{Proxy ethnicity, education: the assumption fails on class.} Children with the Black-pool names had
        35--44 points fewer mothers with college. Whatever the RM reads from that, the proxy ethnicity contrast
        carries it: it is ethnicity plus the names' SES signal (people's race perception of a name follows the
        mothers' education, \citealp{gaddis2017lakisha}; Crabtree et al.\ find Black-signalling names perceived
        with less income and education). The explicit ethnicity clause has no names, so explicit vs.\ proxy is the
        in-design comparison. The proxy economic axis (school poverty) is a class cue too, so the proxy
        ethnicity $\times$ economic interaction is read with this in mind.
  \item Sex ambiguity (same-sex share of births $<0.95$): Logan 0.93, DaShawn 0.94, Latrell 0.87. Minor.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limits.} SSA counts end in 2017 (Logan, Claire, Abigail, Hunter were still near their peak; later
medians with newer data, on both sides of the sex contrast). Births, not the living population. Claire is bimodal
(IQR 1947--2008). Gaddis's counts are one state, and tiny for Lakisha (22) and Latonya (15).

\textbf{Open (not decided; after the review):} (i) record as a caveat on every education proxy-ethnicity number;
(ii) swap to SES-matched Black names from Gaddis's set (e.g.\ Jabari 56.8, Nia 64.3, Malia 60.0, Jalen 55.7 \% some
college) --- which Gaddis finds are perceived as Black less consistently, a trade-off between race-signal
strength and class balance; (iii) measure the RM's sensitivity directly with a within-race name contrast (high-
vs.\ low-SES names, same sex) and a within-sex cohort contrast (young vs.\ old names), which turns the gaps above
into a bound on the bias. (iii) needs new pairs, so it goes with the pilot re-run.

\subsection*{2026-09-27 --- code review, probes/: cross\_marker\_directions.py}

  \item \textbf{Info, no change:} a record id missing from the state index raises a bare \texttt{KeyError}

\subsection*{2026-09-28 --- code review, scoring/: cross\_marker\_metrics.py}

\begin{itemize}

        deliberately acc(B) $-$ acc(A) (positive: the marker costs accuracy), as documented. With the datasets

        pinned against the reference. Ties count $\tfrac12$ in accuracy and every AUC; the AUC bootstrap by

\subsection*{2026-09-29 --- code review, runners/: run\_additivity.py}
RQ1.1 in state space (188 lines). Mechanically sound (all four directions on the same probe records, per-record
contrasts, a record bootstrap), but it did not measure what RQ1.1 defines, and its verdict misled. Checked with a
scratch run of Qwen3-0.6B on credit explicit (150 records, MPS; a check of the code, not a result). Seven findings,
all fixed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(1, medium) The corner was compared with the sum of the \emph{unit} marginal directions.} Under
        additivity the all-A-vs-all-B contrast equals the \emph{vector} sum of the marginal contrasts (RQ1.1's own
        wording); normalising first over-weights the shorter contrasts and always biases the cosine down. A perfectly
        additive synthetic design with one contrast three times the others: 1.000 against the vector sum, 0.886
        against the unit sum (``partially additive''). Qwen, credit: the contrast lengths differ (age 1.55, sex
        1.43, marital 0.73), 0.9994 against 0.958. Now the vector sum.
  \item \textbf{(2, medium; reading rule, carried forward from \texttt{pairs/}/\texttt{scoring/}) The test cannot see
        two-way intersectionality.} In effects coding the corner is $2\sum$ mains $+ 2\times$ the three-way term; the
        two-way terms cancel, as in reward space. Checked numerically: a strong two-way (``young women'') term leaves
        the cosine at 1.000. So a high cosine means ``no three-way structure in state space'', not ``additive /
        low-complexity''; two-way intersectionality is read from the cross-marker design's interactions. Docstring
        and printout say so; decided 2026-09-29 to leave state-space two-way terms out.
  \item \textbf{(3, low--medium) The verdict} (``additive'' $\geq 0.9$, ``partially'' $\geq 0.6$, never
        pre-registered, read off the point estimate) is gone. \textbf{Its replacement changed once during the fix.}
        The first version gave the cosine and a residual share bootstrap intervals and a reliability ceiling; the
        scratch run showed both invalid: the intervals missed their own estimates (cosine 0.9994, ``interval''
        $[0.9989, 0.9993]$; residual 0.034, $[0.038, 0.047]$), because resampling noise lengthens a residual and
        shortens a cosine; and the cosine exceeded its ceiling, which assumes independent noise while the corner and
        the marginals share cell states. \textbf{Decided 2026-09-29:} the per-record residual $r = $ corner $-\ \Sigma$
        marginals (its mean estimates twice the three-way term) is reported as a share of the corner's length ---
        raw, the noise floor $\sqrt{\operatorname{tr} S/n}$, and debiased $\sqrt{\max(0, \|\bar r\|^2 -
        \operatorname{tr} S/n)}$ --- and ``no three-way term'' is tested by sign flips of the records' residuals
        (valid if each is symmetric about 0). The cosine is a point value; the pairwise marginal cosines keep
        bootstrap intervals (not length-type; they cover their estimates). On synthetic designs: additive and strong
        two-way both debiased 0 and $p = 0.90$; a small three-way term $p = 0.0002$; pure noise $p$ roughly uniform
        (a test). The scratch run: raw 0.034, floor 0.025, debiased 0.023, $p = 0.0001$ --- most of the raw residual
        is noise, a small three-way component remains.
  \item \textbf{(4, low) Two estimates of one number} (the unit probe directions, the per-record means): one now.
  \item \textbf{(5, medium) Provenance, names, CLI:} \texttt{meta} with the manifest checked before loading; always
