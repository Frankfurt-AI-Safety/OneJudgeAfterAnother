\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Design grammar.} Every effect is measured on matched pairs that hold content fixed and vary only
the target attribute, so content-driven activation cancels in the difference. We report two metrics
\citep{kumar2025prefix}: \autoinf{} (preference shift on equal-quality items) and \crossinf{} (accuracy
degradation when the attribute is attached to the weaker option, a reliability harm that maps onto the Act's
concern with biased false positives and negatives). Each attribute is tested in an \emph{explicit} (stated)
and a \emph{proxy} (inferred) encoding. \paragraph{Mechanistic step.} From the matched pairs we estimate a
\diffmean{} direction and sweep the null-space projection strength $\alpha$; a bias is \emph{reward-scalar}
low-complexity if nulling removes it at near-zero cost to legitimate accuracy. \paragraph{Representation-level
test.} Because null-space projection and a linear probe only interrogate the linear subspace, we additionally
apply \leace{} (optimal linear erasure) and then train a non-linear (MLP) probe on the erased
representation. A concept that is linearly erasable but non-linearly recoverable is entangled
(high-complexity) regardless of what the reward scalar shows --- the discriminator at the heart of this work.
