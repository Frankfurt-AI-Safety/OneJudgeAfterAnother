\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The reward model is the most reused and least directly observed component of the alignment stack. A single
preference model, trained once from human comparisons, is subsequently consulted as the arbiter of quality
across RLHF policy optimization, best-of-$N$ inference-time selection, iterative rejection-sampling data
filtering, and automated evaluation. A systematic error in that one component is therefore not a local defect
but an upstream point of failure whose effects surface, and compound, across many downstream behaviors. The
EU AI Act's high-risk regime (Annex~III) names recruitment and creditworthiness as high-risk uses and
requires technical robustness against biased outcomes on protected characteristics \citep{euaiact2024}, yet
most fairness auditing targets the deployed policy's outputs rather than the evaluator that shaped them.
\citet{fein2026onebias} supply a mechanistic instrument that, for capability- and style-related biases,
distinguishes biases removable by a cheap post-hoc edit from those entangled with legitimate signal, but they
never apply it to protected attributes. This paper carries that low- versus high-complexity lens into the
protected-attribute setting and strengthens it with a concept-erasure robustness check.

\paragraph{Research questions.}
\textbf{RQ0} (replication): do the base-paper interventions reproduce, and does the stronger
\leace{}/non-linear-probe test alter the low/high-complexity verdict? \textbf{RQ1}: which EU-protected
attributes does an \rewardmodel{} exhibit a measurable bias for on otherwise-identical high-risk content, and
is the intersectional effect distinct and non-additive? \textbf{RQ2}: is that bias low- or high-complexity,
distinguishing the reward-scalar level from the representation level? \textbf{RQ3}: does mechanistic reward
shaping reduce the harm without degrading legitimate accuracy? \textbf{RQ4}: is the removal robust, or does
the concept leak back --- the reward-vs-representation gap?
