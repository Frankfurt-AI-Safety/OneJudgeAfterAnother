\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{The reward-vs-representation gap.} Our central mechanistic finding combines two measurements that,
at present, sit on different concepts. First, demographic bias is behaviourally removable: null-space
projection zeroes its contribution to the (linear) reward head across all three domains. Second, on the
model-response arm, the reasoning-correctness concept that dominates verdict scores survives \emph{optimal}
linear erasure and is still recovered by a non-linear probe at near-ceiling accuracy. Should the same hold for
the demographic directions --- the test we have not yet run, and the one we run next --- then debiasing the
reward is not the same as debiasing the model. For compliance-grade mitigation the implication is a caution: a
cheap post-hoc edit can make an evaluator appear unbiased on the metric while the discriminating information
remains available to any downstream non-linear computation, so the \emph{robustness} of the intervention, not
its immediate effect, is the load-bearing question.
\paragraph{Limitations.} Results so far are on a single 0.6B RM at a single seed, and use templated
matched-pair items; the scaling study across eleven RMs up to 70B, and the standpoint arm on a topic-natural
corpus, are in progress. We report no confidence intervals or significance tests, and sample sizes vary by arm
(\crossNCredit{} down to \nEvalStandpointAsap{}), so small differences between cells should not be read as
real. Our headline \autoinf{} statistic is a preference \emph{rate} and therefore saturates: a third of the
battery cells sit at ceiling, which makes them uninformative about effect \emph{size}; the standpoint arm's
identity gap, measured in raw reward units against a no-standpoint baseline, is the magnitude-preserving
readout and we prefer it where available. The hiring substrate is synthetic, and it is the substrate carrying
the erasure, reasoning, and decision arms. The accuracy guardrail (RewardBench-2 non-inferiority) is not yet
reported, and on credit and CV the RM does not track quality at all, so no in-domain accuracy trade-off can be
measured there. Standpoint probe accuracies are near chance, so the standpoint \emph{nulling} results are
weaker evidence than the standpoint \emph{gap} results. Finally, our markers are injected rather than
naturally occurring: on the one arm where we can compare, the real-field direction is anti-correlated with the
synthetic one, which we are following up as a validity question in its own right.
\paragraph{Future work.} Beyond the scaling study: extending the erasure test to the demographic directions;
a cross-domain transfer test for leak-back; and a forced-choice pairwise readout as a cross-check on the
scalar one, which needs a generative judge since it is out-of-distribution for a scalar RM.
