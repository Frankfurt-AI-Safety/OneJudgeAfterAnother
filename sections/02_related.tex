\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{The base paper.} \citet{fein2026onebias} measure biases in high-quality RMs and classify failures
by complexity: low-complexity biases are approximately linear directions removed by a difference-of-means
probe plus null-space projection, whereas high-complexity biases (sycophancy, model-style) are co-linear with
useful signal and resist single-direction removal. \paragraph{Demographic bias in RMs.} \citet{kumar2025prefix}
introduce prefix bias and the auto- and cross-influence metrics; \citet{song2025groupfairness} study
group-level demographic parity across RMs. Neither asks whether the bias is linearly removable.
\paragraph{Concept erasure.} \leace{} \citep{belrose2023leace} is a closed-form, provably optimal linear
erasure; the \taco{} result \citep{taco2024} shows a concept can survive optimal linear erasure yet remain
recoverable by a non-linear probe, which we adopt as the operational signature of entanglement; the lineage
traces to iterative null-space projection \citep{ravfogel2020nullitout}. \paragraph{Audit design.} Our
matched-pair construction follows the correspondence-audit lineage \citep{tamkin2023discrimeval,haim2024whatsinaname}.
\paragraph{Gap.} No prior work applies the mechanistic linear / null-space lens to protected-attribute bias
in the reward model itself.
